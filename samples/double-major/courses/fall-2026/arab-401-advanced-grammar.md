## the course

ARAB 401, advanced Arabic grammar (النَّحْو, al-naḥw). Dr. Saeed. MWF 10:10–11:00, Hamdan 214. Office hours Tue 2–4 and by appointment (she actually answers email, use it).

Prereqs were ARAB 302 (ṣarf, Prof. Haddad, spring) and 301. She assumes the verb forms and the weak-verb paradigms cold, so anything that goes shaky there goes back into the 302 notes, not here.

Course description from the syllabus, paraphrased: a systematic study of the nawāsiḵ (the "abrogators" that enter a nominal sentence and change its case marking), the manṣūbāt (the accusative complements: ḥāl, tamyīz, the five mafʿūls), conditional and oath constructions, and the theory of iʿrāb as the classical grammarians framed it. Weekly parsing (iʿrāb) of short passages from classical prose, the Qurʾān and modern newspaper Arabic.

## grading

Weekly iʿrāb sheets 25%, two midterms 30% (first one was Wed 10/21, got it back: 88, lost most points on lā al-nāfiya li-l-jins with a dual ism, see the list below), final 30%, participation and recitation 15%. Recitation means reading a vocalized passage aloud with correct case endings. She stops you on every wrong ending.

## the textbook

R. Malouf & S. Idris, *A Course in Arabic Syntax*, 3rd ed. — we follow chapters 9–17. The chapters are good on the lists and the examples but thin on why; Dr. Saeed's lectures add the classical reasoning (Sībawayh, Ibn Hišām's *Muġnī*, Ibn ʿAqīl on the *Alfiyya*). When the book and lecture disagree on terminology I write the lecture's term and put the book's in brackets.

Reference I actually keep open: the iʿrāb section at the back of the book, and Wright's grammar (vol. II) for the classical examples. Wright is old but public domain and nobody has beaten its index.

## iʿrāb mistakes I keep making

A running list. Every time a sheet comes back with red on it, the mistake goes here first, and if it happens twice it becomes a card.

1. Putting the ḵabar of laysa in the nominative when it has the bāʾ: لَسْتُ بِغَافِلٍ. The ḵabar is majrūr lafẓan (the bāʾ is zāʾida), manṣūb maḥallan. I keep writing "marfūʿ". (sheet 2)
2. Treating the ism of kāna as fāʿil. It is ism kāna, marfūʿ. Calling it fāʿil loses the point even if the case is right. (sheet 1, and again in recitation)
3. Sound feminine plural in the accusative takes kasra, not fatḥa: لَيْسَتِ الطَّالِبَاتُ غَائِبَاتٍ, not غَائِبَاتًا. Obvious on paper, I still say it wrong aloud. (recitation 9/18)
4. After inna, the ḵabar stays marfūʿ. I hyper-correct to manṣūb because "inna makes things manṣūb". (sheet 4)
5. lā al-nāfiya li-l-jins with a dual ism: لَا طَالِبَيْنِ فِي القَاعَةِ, mabnī ʿalā l-yāʾ, not manṣūb muʿrab. Midterm 1 Q6. (lost 4 points)
6. Reading iḏā as jāzima. It isn't (except in poetry). The verb after it is māḍī in form and future in meaning. (just lectured, not yet tested)
7. Tamyīz after 11–99 is singular manṣūb, never plural: ثَلَاثَةَ عَشَرَ كِتَابًا. I wrote كُتُبًا on sheet 6. Embarrassing.
8. Forgetting that the ḥāl can be a whole sentence with wāw al-ḥāl, and then trying to parse the wāw as ʿaṭf. (sheet 6)
