## the parts

A conditional sentence (الجُمْلَة الشَّرْطِيَّة) has three parts: the particle or noun of condition (أَدَاة الشَّرْط), the condition clause (فِعْل الشَّرْط), and the answer or apodosis (جَوَاب الشَّرْط, also جَزَاء).

إِنْ تَدْرُسْ تَنْجَحْ, in tadrus tanjaḥ, "if you study, you'll succeed".

in: adāt al-šarṭ. tadrus: fiʿl al-šarṭ, muḍāriʿ majzūm bi-l-sukūn. tanjaḥ: jawāb al-šarṭ, muḍāriʿ majzūm bi-l-sukūn.

Dr. Saeed's main division: the tools that put both verbs in the jussive (jāzima) and those that don't (ġayr jāzima).

the three parts of a conditional sentence are adāt al-šarṭ, {{c1::fiʿl al-šarṭ}}, and {{c2::jawāb al-šarṭ}} ^nh-shart-parts

## jāzima: two jussives

Particles: إِنْ in, إِذْمَا iḏmā (rare, "she has never seen it outside a grammar book").

Nouns (asmāʾ al-šarṭ), which also have a function inside their clause:

| tool | translit. | meaning | its own iʿrāb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| مَنْ | man | whoever | depends: mubtadaʾ, mafʿūl… |
| مَا | mā | whatever | same |
| مَهْمَا | mahmā | whatever (stronger) | same |
| مَتَى | matā | whenever | ẓarf zamān |
| أَيَّانَ | ayyāna | whenever | ẓarf zamān |
| أَيْنَ / أَيْنَمَا | ayna(mā) | wherever | ẓarf makān |
| حَيْثُمَا | ḥayṯumā | wherever | ẓarf makān |
| أَنَّى | annā | wherever, however | ẓarf makān |
| أَيّ | ayy | whichever | depends on what it is added to |

مَنْ يَجْتَهِدْ يَنْجَحْ, man yajtahid yanjaḥ, "whoever works hard succeeds". man: ism šarṭ jāzim mabnī ʿalā l-sukūn fī maḥall rafʿ mubtadaʾ. [and the ḵabar of man? she said three views: the šarṭ clause, the jawāb, or both. "Pick one and say which." Most modern textbooks: the šarṭ+jawāb together. ours: fiʿl al-šarṭ. CHECK book §15.3]

مَا تَفْعَلْ مِنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدْهُ, "whatever good you do, you'll find it". mā: fī maḥall naṣb mafʿūl bihi muqaddam for tafʿal.

أَيْنَمَا تَذْهَبْ أَذْهَبْ مَعَكَ, "wherever you go I'll go with you".

The verbs in the jussive: the sign changes with the verb type, which is 302 again. Hollow: إِنْ تَقُمْ أَقُمْ (long vowel drops). Defective: مَنْ يَسْعَ يَصِلْ (final weak letter drops: yasʿā → yasʿa; and yaṣil is assimilated, lost its wāw already in the imperfect). Five verbs: إِنْ تَدْرُسُوا تَنْجَحُوا (nūn drops).

When the verbs are māḍī, they don't change form but are fī maḥall jazm: إِنْ دَرَسْتَ نَجَحْتَ. Any combination of māḍī and muḍāriʿ is allowed; muḍāriʿ in the jawāb after a māḍī šarṭ may be majzūm or marfūʿ: إِنْ دَرَسْتَ تَنْجَحْ / تَنْجَحُ. [she said the rafʿ is "good Arabic", Sībawayh allows it]

مَنْ يَجْتَهِدْ {{c1::يَنْجَحْ}} — the jawāb is majzūm too ^nh-man-yajtahid

مَنْ يَسْعَ يَصِلْ — why is it يَسْعَ and not يَسْعَى?

because yasʿā is defective: in the jussive the final weak letter (alif maqṣūra) drops, and the fatḥa before it is left ^nh-yasa

## fāʾ al-jawāb

The fāʾ is required when the jawāb cannot itself be a fiʿl al-šarṭ, i.e. when it is not a plain verb that could be jussive. Then the jawāb is a jumla in maḥall jazm, introduced by the fāʾ (الفَاءُ رَابِطَةٌ لِجَوَابِ الشَّرْطِ).

The verse she wrote on the board (copied as I heard it; I may have the order of the second half wrong):

اسْمِيَّةٌ طَلَبِيَّةٌ وَبِجَامِدٍ ... وَبِمَا وَلَنْ وَبِقَدْ وَبِالتَّنْفِيسِ

= the fāʾ is needed when the jawāb is:

| case | example |
| --- | --- |
| a nominal sentence (ismiyya) | إِنْ تَدْرُسْ فَأَنْتَ نَاجِحٌ |
| a request (ṭalabiyya: imperative, prohibition, question…) | إِنْ جَاءَ زَيْدٌ فَأَكْرِمْهُ |
| a jāmid verb (laysa, ʿasā, niʿma, biʾsa) | إِنْ تَكْسَلْ فَلَيْسَ لَكَ عُذْرٌ |
| negated with mā | إِنْ تَتَأَخَّرْ فَمَا أَنْتَظِرُكَ |
| negated with lan | إِنْ تَكْذِبْ فَلَنْ أُصَدِّقَكَ |
| with qad | إِنْ يَسْرِقْ فَقَدْ سَرَقَ أَخٌ لَهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ (Yūsuf 12:77) |
| with sa- / sawfa (tanfīs) | إِنْ تَجْتَهِدْ فَسَوْفَ تَنْجَحُ |

In إِنْ تَدْرُسْ فَأَنْتَ نَاجِحٌ: al-fāʾ rābiṭa li-jawāb al-šarṭ; anta mubtadaʾ, nājiḥun ḵabar; wa-l-jumla l-ismiyya fī maḥall jazm jawāb al-šarṭ.

Note that with lan the verb stays subjunctive (uṣaddiqaka), with sawfa indicative (tanjaḥu): the jawāb is no longer a jussive verb, it is a clause in the place of one.

the fāʾ al-jawāb is required when the jawāb is {{c1::a nominal sentence, a request, a jāmid verb, or begins with mā, lan, qad, or sa-/sawfa}} ^nh-fa-jawab
<!-- sr st=new -->

إِنْ تَكْذِبْ فَلَنْ أُصَدِّقَكَ — why the fāʾ, and why is the verb subjunctive?
?
the jawāb begins with lan, so it cannot be a jussive verb itself and needs the fāʾ; lan puts uṣaddiqa in the subjunctive, and the whole clause is fī maḥall jazm as jawāb al-šarṭ ^nh-lan-fa

## ġayr jāzima

### iḏā

إِذَا, iḏā: "when, if" for the expected future. Followed usually by a māḍī with future meaning. It is a ẓarf for the future containing the meaning of condition, mabnī ʿalā l-sukūn fī maḥall naṣb, and does NOT put the verbs in the jussive.

إِذَا جَاءَ الصَّيْفُ سَافَرْنَا, iḏā jāʾa ṣ-ṣayfu sāfarnā, "when summer comes, we'll travel".

The fāʾ rules apply to its jawāb too: إِذَا جَاءَ زَيْدٌ فَأَكْرِمْهُ.

in vs iḏā: in is for the doubtful ("if, maybe"), iḏā for the certain or likely ("when"). إِذَا مِتُّ, not إِنْ مِتُّ, unless you are being poetic about immortality. [her joke]

إِذَا ::: when/if for the expected future; ẓarf, not jāzima ^nh-idha

### law

لَوْ, law: the counterfactual. ḥarf imtināʿ li-mtināʿ, "a particle of impossibility for impossibility": the jawāb did not happen because the šarṭ did not. Usually with māḍī verbs, and the jawāb takes lām when positive:

لَوْ دَرَسْتَ لَنَجَحْتَ, law darasta la-najaḥta, "if you had studied, you would have passed".

The lām is lām wāqiʿa fī jawāb law. Optional, common in positive jawābs, absent in negative ones with mā: لَوْ دَرَسْتَ مَا رَسَبْتَ.

law + anna: لَوْ أَنَّكَ دَرَسْتَ لَنَجَحْتَ (from the inna unit).

law is {{c1::ḥarf imtināʿ li-mtināʿ}}: the jawāb fails because the šarṭ failed ^nh-law
