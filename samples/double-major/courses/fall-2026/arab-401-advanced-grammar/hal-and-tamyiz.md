## why together

Both are manṣūbāt, both are usually indefinite, both are faḍla (extra: the sentence is complete without them), and both answer a question the sentence leaves open. Dr. Saeed taught them in one block because students confuse them constantly, and the test of which one you have is the same each time: what question does the word answer? The ḥāl answers kayfa, "in what state?". The tamyīz answers "of what? in what respect?".

جَاءَ زَيْدٌ رَاكِبًا, "Zayd came riding" (how did he come? riding: ḥāl).

طَابَ زَيْدٌ نَفْسًا, "Zayd was good in spirit / cheerful" (good in what respect? in soul: tamyīz).

Same shape, nakira manṣūba after a complete sentence. Different job.

# al-ḥāl

## definition

الحَالُ: وَصْفٌ فَضْلَةٌ مَنْصُوبٌ يُبَيِّنُ هَيْئَةَ صَاحِبِهِ وَقْتَ وُقُوعِ الفِعْلِ.

"The ḥāl is a description, an extra element, manṣūb, that shows the state (hayʾa) of its owner at the time the action takes place." Each word of the definition does work:

waṣf: normally a derived adjective (muštaqq), active or passive participle, ṣifa mušabbaha. رَاكِبًا, مُسْرِعًا, مَسْرُورًا.

faḍla: you could delete it and still have a sentence.

manṣūb: always, with whatever sign of naṣb the word takes.

hayʾa: the state, not a permanent quality. "He came riding" is about how he was when he came, not what he is.

ṣāḥib al-ḥāl: the noun whose state it describes, normally definite. Can be the fāʿil, the mafʿūl, a majrūr, or the mubtadaʾ in some cases.

## definite ḥāl, indefinite ṣāḥib

The ḥāl is indefinite. When a ḥāl looks definite it is interpreted as indefinite: جَاءُوا وَحْدَهُمْ? no — the textbook's example is اُدْخُلُوا الأَوَّلَ فَالأَوَّلَ, "enter one by one" (= murattabīna), and جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَحْدَهُ, "Zayd came alone" (= munfaridan). waḥdahu has a pronoun suffix and is still a ḥāl.

The ṣāḥib is definite. It can be indefinite only with a justification (musawwiġ), the most common being:

The ḥāl comes before it: فِي الدَّارِ جَالِسًا رَجُلٌ, "in the house, sitting, is a man". [old textbook example; nobody writes this]

The indefinite noun is specified by a ṣifa or iḍāfa: جَاءَنِي رَجُلٌ كَرِيمٌ مُبْتَسِمًا.

After negation or a question: مَا جَاءَ أَحَدٌ إِلَّا رَاكِبًا.

The reason, as Dr. Saeed put it: if the ṣāḥib is indefinite and the descriptive word follows it, an adjective (naʿt) is the more natural reading and the ḥāl reading is lost. رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا رَاكِبًا is "I saw a riding man" (naʿt), not "I saw a man, riding". With a definite ṣāḥib the two are distinguished by the article: رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَ الرَّاكِبَ (naʿt) vs رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَ رَاكِبًا (ḥāl).

## the shapes of the ḥāl

**mufrad** (a single word, not a sentence; can be dual or plural): all the examples so far.

**jumla ismiyya**: جَاءَ الطَّالِبُ وَهُوَ يَبْتَسِمُ, jāʾa ṭ-ṭālibu wa-huwa yabtasimu, "the student came smiling" (lit. "and he is smiling"). The wāw is wāw al-ḥāl, and the clause wa-huwa yabtasimu is jumla ismiyya fī maḥall naṣb ḥāl.

**jumla fiʿliyya**: جَاءَ الطَّالِبُ يَبْتَسِمُ, "the student came, smiling". yabtasimu + its hidden fāʿil is jumla fiʿliyya fī maḥall naṣb ḥāl.

**shibh jumla**: رَأَيْتُ الهِلَالَ بَيْنَ السَّحَابِ, raʾaytu l-hilāla bayna s-saḥābi, "I saw the crescent among the clouds". The ẓarf is mutaʿalliq with a deleted ḥāl (kāʾinan).

The sentence-ḥāl needs a rābiṭ linking it to the ṣāḥib: the wāw, a pronoun, or both.

| type of jumla ḥāliyya | rābiṭ | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ismiyya | wāw + pronoun, or wāw alone | جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَهُوَ يَضْحَكُ / جِئْتُ وَالشَّمْسُ طَالِعَةٌ |
| fiʿliyya, muḍāriʿ muṯbat (positive) | pronoun only, no wāw | جَاءَ زَيْدٌ يَضْحَكُ |
| fiʿliyya, muḍāriʿ negated with lā | pronoun only | جَاءَ زَيْدٌ لَا يَتَكَلَّمُ |
| fiʿliyya, māḍī | wāw + qad (usually) | جَاءَ زَيْدٌ وَقَدْ غَرَبَتِ الشَّمْسُ |

The wāw al-ḥāl is parsed: الوَاوُ وَاوُ الحَالِ، حَرْفٌ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى الفَتْحِ لَا مَحَلَّ لَهُ مِنَ الإِعْرَابِ. Then the jumla after it fī maḥall naṣb ḥāl.

## the jāmid ḥāl

The ḥāl is normally muštaqq. It can be jāmid (underived) when it can be interpreted as a derived word (muʾawwal bi-muštaqq), in a handful of patterns:

Price / exchange: بِعْتُهُ يَدًا بِيَدٍ, "I sold it hand to hand" (= mutaqābiḍayni, immediate exchange).

Comparison: كَرَّ زَيْدٌ أَسَدًا, "Zayd charged like a lion" (= mušbihan asadan).

Order / arrangement: اُدْخُلُوا رَجُلًا رَجُلًا, "enter man by man".

And when it is not interpretable as derived but is described: سَمِعْتُ صَوْتَهَا قَوِيًّا is fine; وَأَرْسَلْنَاكَ لِلنَّاسِ رَسُولًا (al-Nisāʾ 4:79), "We sent you to mankind as a messenger": rasūlan is a ḥāl muʾakkida, a ḥāl that confirms the meaning of the verb. # al-tamyīz

## definition

التَّمْيِيزُ: اسْمٌ نَكِرَةٌ مَنْصُوبٌ يُزِيلُ الإِبْهَامَ عَمَّا قَبْلَهُ.

"The tamyīz (specification) is an indefinite noun, manṣūb, that removes the vagueness (ibhām) of what comes before it." Usually jāmid, where the ḥāl is usually muštaqq. Two kinds, by what is vague.

## tamyīz al-ḏāt (al-malfūẓ): the vague thing is a word

The vague word is right there, said aloud (malfūẓ), and it is a quantity: a number, a measure, a weight, an area, a volume.

Numbers 11–99: اشْتَرَيْتُ عِشْرِينَ كِتَابًا, ištaraytu ʿišrīna kitāban, "I bought twenty books". The tamyīz of 11–99 is singular and manṣūb. ثَلَاثَةَ عَشَرَ طَالِبًا, ṯalāṯata ʿašara ṭāliban, "thirteen students". [mistake #7: كُتُبًا is wrong, singular only]

Contrast: 3–10 take a plural majrūr noun in iḍāfa (ثَلَاثَةُ كُتُبٍ), and 100 and 1000 take a singular majrūr in iḍāfa (مِائَةُ كِتَابٍ, أَلْفُ طَالِبٍ). Neither of those is tamyīz in the strict sense; they are muḍāf ilayhi. Some grammarians call them tamyīz majrūr; Dr. Saeed wants "muḍāf ilayhi" on sheets.

Weights and measures: عِنْدِي رِطْلٌ زَيْتًا, "I have a pound of oil"; اشْتَرَيْتُ مِتْرًا قُمَاشًا, "I bought a meter of cloth"; شَرِبْتُ كُوبًا حَلِيبًا. All of these may also be said with min (رِطْلٌ مِنْ زَيْتٍ) or in iḍāfa (رِطْلُ زَيْتٍ). With min, the noun is majrūr by min and the iʿrāb is just "ism majrūr".

After kam al-istifhāmiyya: كَمْ كِتَابًا قَرَأْتَ؟, kam kitāban qaraʾta?, "how many books did you read?". The tamyīz of interrogative kam is singular manṣūb. [kam al-ḵabariyya, "how many…!", takes a majrūr noun, singular or plural: كَمْ كِتَابٍ قَرَأْتُ! — separate, next week]

## tamyīz al-nisba (al-malḥūẓ): the vague thing is the relation

Here no single word is vague. The vagueness is in the relation (nisba) of the verb to its subject or object: in what respect is it true? The vagueness is "noticed", not said (malḥūẓ).

طَابَ زَيْدٌ نَفْسًا, ṭāba zaydun nafsan, "Zayd was cheerful" (good in spirit). The statement "Zayd was good" is vague: good how? nafsan specifies.

ازْدَادَ الطَّالِبُ عِلْمًا, izdāda ṭ-ṭālibu ʿilman, "the student increased in knowledge".

وَفَجَّرْنَا الأَرْضَ عُيُونًا (al-Qamar 54:12), "and We made the earth burst forth in springs". ʿuyūnan is tamyīz from the mafʿūl.

Dr. Saeed's trick: a tamyīz al-nisba is "converted" (muḥawwal) from a fāʿil or a mafʿūl. طَابَ زَيْدٌ نَفْسًا ← طَابَتْ نَفْسُ زَيْدٍ, "Zayd's soul was good". فَجَّرْنَا الأَرْضَ عُيُونًا ← فَجَّرْنَا عُيُونَ الأَرْضِ. If you can rewrite it that way, it is tamyīz al-nisba muḥawwal ʿan al-fāʿil / al-mafʿūl.

With the elative (ism al-tafḍīl), which can't make a comparison of something abstract by itself: أَنْتَ أَكْثَرُ مِنِّي مَالًا, anta akṯaru minnī mālan, "you have more money than I" (more in respect of money). This is also how you build the elative of colors and of derived-form verbs, which can't take the afʿal pattern directly: أَشَدُّ حُمْرَةً, "redder" (more intense in redness); أَكْثَرُ اسْتِخْدَامًا, "more used". ## telling them apart

| | ḥāl | tamyīz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| part of speech | waṣf (usually muštaqq) | ism (usually jāmid) |
| answers | kayfa? in what state? | of what? in what respect? |
| can be a sentence | yes (jumla ḥāliyya) | never |
| can be definite in form | rarely (waḥdahu) | never |
| relation to its owner | describes a temporary state | specifies a vague quantity or relation |
| can be repeated, two at once | yes | no |

The edge cases Dr. Saeed threw at us:

لِلَّهِ دَرُّهُ فَارِسًا!, li-llāhi darruhu fārisan!, "what a horseman he is!". fārisan is muštaqq (active participle), so it looks like a ḥāl. But the sentence is an exclamation of wonder about him in respect of horsemanship, not about his state at a time; the majority say tamyīz.
