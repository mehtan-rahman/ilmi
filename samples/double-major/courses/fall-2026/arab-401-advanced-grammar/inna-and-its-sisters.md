## the mirror image of kāna

إِنَّ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا (inna wa-aḵawātuhā) are particles, not verbs, and they do the opposite of kāna: the ism becomes manṣūb, the ḵabar stays marfūʿ.

العِلْمُ نُورٌ ← إِنَّ العِلْمَ نُورٌ

al-ʿilmu nūrun → inna l-ʿilma nūrun, "knowledge is light" → "indeed, knowledge is light".

Dr. Saeed's reason they govern at all: they resemble verbs (they are built on the fatḥ like a māḍī, they have three or more letters, they take attached object pronouns like verbs do: innahu, innanī). The grammarians call them al-aḥruf al-mušabbaha bi-l-fiʿl, "the particles resembling the verb". And since a verb puts its fāʿil before its object in the usual order, these particles are like a verb whose object comes first: manṣūb, then marfūʿ. [she called it "a verb with the object fronted", which is a mnemonic, not an argument; she said so herself]

inna and its sisters make the {{c1::ism manṣūb}} and leave the {{c2::ḵabar marfūʿ}} ^nh-inna-rule

## the six

| particle | translit. | meaning | function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| إِنَّ | inna | indeed, truly | tawkīd (emphasis) |
| أَنَّ | anna | that | tawkīd; makes a maṣdar muʾawwal |
| كَأَنَّ | ka-anna | as if, as though | tašbīh (comparison), sometimes ẓann (supposition) |
| لَكِنَّ | lākinna | but | istidrāk (correction of an expectation) |
| لَيْتَ | layta | if only, would that | tamannī (wishing, usually the impossible or unlikely) |
| لَعَلَّ | laʿalla | perhaps, so that maybe | tarajjī (hoping for the possible) or tawaqquʿ / išfāq |

Examples, my own except where marked:

إِنَّ الامْتِحَانَ قَرِيبٌ, inna l-imtiḥāna qarībun, "the exam is close (truly)".

عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ الامْتِحَانَ قَرِيبٌ, ʿalimtu anna l-imtiḥāna qarībun, "I learned that the exam is close".

كَأَنَّ المَدِينَةَ لَوْحَةٌ, ka-anna l-madīnata lawḥatun, "it is as if the city were a painting".

الطَّقْسُ بَارِدٌ لَكِنَّ الشَّمْسَ سَاطِعَةٌ, aṭ-ṭaqsu bāridun lākinna š-šamsa sāṭiʿatun, "the weather is cold, but the sun is shining".

لَيْتَ الشَّبَابَ يَعُودُ, layta š-šabāba yaʿūdu, "if only youth would return". (the famous half-line is لَيْتَ الشَّبَابَ يَعُودُ يَوْمًا; Dr. Saeed quoted it, I'm not sure of the poet [Abū l-ʿAtāhiya? check], keep the half-line only)

لَعَلَّ الحَافِلَةَ تَصِلُ قَرِيبًا, laʿalla l-ḥāfilata taṣilu qarīban, "perhaps the bus will arrive soon".

The difference between layta and laʿalla is the whole point of the pair: layta wishes for what will not or cannot happen (youth returning), laʿalla hopes for what may. "laytanī najaḥtu" = I wish I had passed (I didn't); "laʿallī anjaḥu" = maybe I'll pass.

layta expresses {{c1::tamannī, wishing for the impossible or unlikely}}, laʿalla expresses {{c2::tarajjī, hoping for the possible}} ^nh-layta-laalla

## with pronouns

The ism is often an attached pronoun, which is where the forms get irregular.

| | inna | anna | layta | laʿalla | lākinna |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1sg | إِنِّي / إِنَّنِي | أَنِّي / أَنَّنِي | لَيْتَنِي (laytī, rare) | لَعَلِّي / لَعَلَّنِي | لَكِنِّي / لَكِنَّنِي |
| 1pl | إِنَّا / إِنَّنَا | أَنَّا / أَنَّنَا | لَيْتَنَا | لَعَلَّنَا | لَكِنَّا / لَكِنَّنَا |
| 3sg m | إِنَّهُ | أَنَّهُ | لَيْتَهُ | لَعَلَّهُ | لَكِنَّهُ |

The extra nūn in innanī is nūn al-wiqāya ("the protecting nūn"), which keeps the final fatḥa of the particle from being swallowed by the -ī. Particles ending in a doubled nūn (inna, anna, ka-anna, lākinna) can drop it (innī) because a nūn is already there. layta almost always keeps it; laʿalla almost always drops it (laʿallī). So the two ends of the scale are laytanī and laʿallī.

In iʿrāb: إِنَّنِي: إِنَّ حَرْفُ تَوْكِيدٍ وَنَصْبٍ، وَالنُّونُ لِلْوِقَايَةِ، وَاليَاءُ ضَمِيرٌ مُتَّصِلٌ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى السُّكُونِ فِي مَحَلِّ نَصْبٍ اسْمُ إِنَّ.

the nūn in إِنَّنِي and لَيْتَنِي is {{c1::nūn al-wiqāya}}, which protects the particle's final vowel before the first-person -ī ^nh-wiqaya

## inna versus anna

This is the topic of the unit. Same meaning (emphasis), same government, different place in the sentence.

**inna (kasra)** opens a sentence that stands on its own: the sentence keeps its status as a sentence. **anna (fatḥa)** turns its clause into the equivalent of a single noun, a maṣdar muʾawwal, which then has to fill some slot in a bigger sentence: subject, object, object of a preposition.

Test from lecture: can you replace "anna + ism + ḵabar" with a maṣdar? عَلِمْتُ أَنَّكَ مُسَافِرٌ = عَلِمْتُ سَفَرَكَ, "I learned of your traveling". Yes → anna. If the slot needs a whole sentence, inna.

Where inna is obligatory (the list from the handout, condensed):

At the very beginning of speech: إِنَّ الوَقْتَ ضَيِّقٌ.

After the verb qāla and its derivatives (the quotation is a sentence, not a noun): قَالَ إِنَّ الوَقْتَ ضَيِّقٌ, "he said that time is short". This is the one MSA writers get wrong: you hear qāla anna on the news and it is a mistake.

At the beginning of a ṣila (relative clause): جَاءَ الَّذِي إِنَّهُ صَدِيقُكَ? [no, her example was better, from the Qurʾān: مَا إِنَّ مَفَاتِحَهُ لَتَنُوءُ بِالعُصْبَةِ (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:76). I'm fairly sure of the wording, it has the lām too, which is why she used it]

After an oath: وَاللَّهِ إِنَّ الأَمْرَ جَدٌّ.

After alā (the opening particle): أَلَا إِنَّ…

When the ḵabar has the lām al-muzaḥlaqa (see below), even after a verb of knowing: عَلِمْتُ إِنَّكَ لَصَادِقٌ, ʿalimtu innaka la-ṣādiqun. The lām "suspends" the verb (taʿlīq) and blocks the clause from becoming a noun, so the kasra comes back.

In a ḥāl clause after wāw al-ḥāl: جِئْتُ وَإِنَّ الشَّمْسَ طَالِعَةٌ.

Where anna is obligatory: whenever the clause fills a noun's slot.

As fāʿil: بَلَغَنِي أَنَّكَ مُسَافِرٌ, balaġanī annaka musāfirun, "it reached me that you are traveling" (fāʿil = safaruka).

As mafʿūl bihi: عَرَفْتُ أَنَّ الطَّرِيقَ مُغْلَقٌ.

After a preposition: فَرِحْتُ بِأَنَّكَ نَجَحْتَ, "I was glad that you passed" (majrūr by bi-).

As mubtadaʾ (usually with a fronted ḵabar): عِنْدِي أَنَّكَ مُحِقٌّ, "my view is that you're right".

After law: لَوْ أَنَّكَ دَرَسْتَ لَنَجَحْتَ. [this one belongs to the conditionals too; she said "remember this sentence for later"]

after qāla the particle is {{c1::inna (with kasra)}}, because the quotation is a sentence, not a maṣdar ^nh-qala-inna

?

## lām al-muzaḥlaqa

اللَّامُ المُزَحْلَقَة, al-lām al-muzaḥlaqa, "the lām that slid along". It is a lām of emphasis (lām al-ibtidāʾ) that belongs at the head of the nominal sentence, لَزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ. When inna enters, two emphatic words would sit side by side at the front, which Arabic avoids; so the lām "slides" off the front and lands on the ḵabar, or on the ism if the ism has been delayed:

إِنَّ الصَّبْرَ لَجَمِيلٌ, inna ṣ-ṣabra la-jamīlun, "patience is truly beautiful" (on the ḵabar).

إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً (Āl ʿImrān 3:13), inna fī ḏālika la-ʿibratan, "in that, truly, is a lesson" (on the delayed ism; the ḵabar fī ḏālika comes first).

Rules for where it can land: on the ḵabar if it is not negated and not a past verb without qad (إِنَّهُ لَقَدْ نَجَحَ is fine, إِنَّهُ لَنَجَحَ is not); on the delayed ism; on the shibh jumla or pronoun of separation between them. It goes with inna only, never with anna or the others (some Kūfans allowed it with lākinna; ignore).

iʿrāb: اللَّامُ لَامُ الابْتِدَاءِ المُزَحْلَقَةُ، حَرْفٌ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى الفَتْحِ لَا مَحَلَّ لَهُ مِنَ الإِعْرَابِ. The word it is on keeps its case: la-jamīlun is still ḵabar inna marfūʿ.

the lām al-muzaḥlaqa slides from the front of the sentence onto the ḵabar or the delayed ism because {{c1::two particles of emphasis (inna and the lām) cannot stand together at the front}} ^nh-muzahlaqa

## the ḵabar before the ism

The ḵabar of inna may not come before inna itself (a particle is weaker than a verb; it can only govern what comes after it). It may come between inna and its ism, but only when it is a shibh jumla: إِنَّ فِي البَيْتِ ضَيْفًا, inna fī l-bayti ḍayfan, "there is a guest in the house". And it must come first when the ism is indefinite, same reasoning as with kāna. ḍayfan is ism inna muʾaḵḵar, manṣūb. This is the second-most common error on the sheets: people see the noun at the end and give it ḍamma, because the "ending of the sentence" sounds like a ḵabar.

## mā al-kāffa

When mā is attached to inna and her sisters (innamā, annamā, ka-annamā, lākinnamā, laytamā, laʿallamā), it "stops" (kaffa) them from governing. The sentence goes back to mubtadaʾ marfūʿ + ḵabar marfūʿ. And because the particle is no longer tied to a noun, it can now enter a verbal sentence too.

إِنَّمَا الأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ (ḥadīṯ), innamā l-aʿmālu bi-n-niyyāti, "actions are only by intentions". al-aʿmālu is mubtadaʾ marfūʿ, not ism inna.

إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى اللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ العُلَمَاءُ (Fāṭir 35:28), "only the learned among His servants fear God". innamā + verbal sentence; al-ʿulamāʾu is fāʿil of yaḵšā, delayed. [which is why the meaning flips if you misread the endings: Allāha is manṣūb, the object]

Meaning: innamā is ḥaṣr (restriction): "only".

The exception is laytamā: both are allowed. لَيْتَمَا الشَّبَابَ عَائِدٌ (iʿmāl, mā is zāʾida) and لَيْتَمَا الشَّبَابُ عَائِدٌ (ihmāl, mā is kāffa). Reason given: layta keeps its "verb-likeness" because it still only enters nominal sentences even with mā. [Ibn ʿAqīl, per lecture]

iʿrāb: إِنَّمَا: إِنَّ حَرْفُ تَوْكِيدٍ مَكْفُوفٌ، وَمَا كَافَّةٌ لَا مَحَلَّ لَهَا.

Do not confuse mā al-kāffa with mā mawṣūla written separately: إِنَّ مَا فَعَلْتَهُ خَطَأٌ, inna mā faʿaltahu ḵaṭaʾun, "what you did is a mistake": here mā is the ism of inna (fī maḥall naṣb) and the spelling is separate. Spelling is the clue in a vocalized text; meaning is the clue always.

after mā al-kāffa (إِنَّمَا), the noun that follows is {{c1::mubtadaʾ marfūʿ}}, because mā stops inna from governing ^nh-kaffa

## lightened forms (taḵfīf)

inna and anna and ka-anna and lākinna have "light" (muḵaffafa) forms with a single nūn: in, an, ka-an, lākin.

lākin (light) is a pure particle of istidrāk with no government: الطَّقْسُ بَارِدٌ لَكِنِ الشَّمْسُ سَاطِعَةٌ (kasra on the nūn only to break the two sukūns).

in al-muḵaffafa usually stops governing and then the ḵabar takes a lām to distinguish it from in al-nāfiya: إِنْ زَيْدٌ لَقَائِمٌ, "Zayd is indeed standing"; vs إِنْ زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ which could be "Zayd is not standing". This lām is called al-lām al-fāriqa, "the separating lām". [new term, card it]

an al-muḵaffafa keeps governing a deleted pronoun of the matter (ḍamīr al-šaʾn): عَلِمْتُ أَنْ سَيَنْجَحُ = annahu sa-yanjaḥu. Don't confuse with an al-maṣdariyya that puts a muḍāriʿ in the subjunctive. Test: an after a verb of certain knowledge (ʿalima) = muḵaffafa; after a verb of wanting/hoping = maṣdariyya + subjunctive.

the lām after in al-muḵaffafa (إِنْ زَيْدٌ لَقَائِمٌ) is called {{c1::al-lām al-fāriqa}}: it separates the lightened in from the negative in ^nh-lam-fariqa

## lā al-nāfiya li-l-jins

Dr. Saeed groups it with inna because it governs exactly like inna (ism manṣūb, ḵabar marfūʿ) "by analogy, being the opposite of inna": inna affirms emphatically, lā al-nāfiya li-l-jins denies emphatically. It denies the whole category (jins), not one instance: لَا رَجُلَ فِي الدَّارِ, lā rajula fī d-dāri, "there is no man at all in the house" (not even one). Compare lā working like laysa, لَا رَجُلٌ فِي الدَّارِ, "no man is in the house" (maybe two are), which negates the unit (al-waḥda) and leaves room for bal rajulāni.

Conditions for it to govern:

| condition | example of the failure |
| --- | --- |
| ism and ḵabar are both indefinite | لَا زَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ وَلَا عَمْرٌو — definite, so lā is ignored (and repeated) |
| the ism comes directly after lā | لَا فِي الدَّارِ رَجُلٌ — separated, so lā stops governing and is repeated |
| no preposition before lā | جِئْتُ بِلَا زَادٍ — the bāʾ governs, lā is just "without" |
| it means negation of the whole genus | (context) |

The ism has three cases, and this is the part that is actually hard.

**mufrad** (not muḍāf and not like a muḍāf; can be singular, dual, plural): mabnī, built on whatever it would be manṣūb with, fī maḥall naṣb.

لَا طَالِبَ غَائِبٌ, lā ṭāliba ġāʾibun: ṭāliba mabnī ʿalā l-fatḥ fī maḥall naṣb (no tanwīn).

لَا طَالِبَيْنِ غَائِبَانِ: mabnī ʿalā l-yāʾ (dual). [midterm Q6, I wrote manṣūb bi-l-yāʾ. It is BUILT on the yāʾ.]

لَا مُعَلِّمِينَ غَائِبُونَ: mabnī ʿalā l-yāʾ (sound masc. plural).

لَا طَالِبَاتِ غَائِبَاتٌ: mabnī ʿalā l-kasr, and fatḥ is also allowed (لَا طَالِبَاتَ). Both in the handout.

**muḍāf**: muʿrab, manṣūb, with tanwīn only if… no, a muḍāf never has tanwīn. لَا طَالِبَ عِلْمٍ نَادِمٌ, lā ṭāliba ʿilmin nādimun, "no seeker of knowledge regrets it". ṭāliba is ism lā manṣūb bi-l-fatḥa (muʿrab, because it is muḍāf). The fatḥa looks identical to the mabnī case; the parse is different.

**šabīh bi-l-muḍāf** ("like a muḍāf": its meaning is completed by something after it, an object or a prepositional phrase it governs): muʿrab, manṣūb, with tanwīn. The classical example: لَا طَالِعًا جَبَلًا حَاضِرٌ, lā ṭāliʿan jabalan ḥāḍirun, "no one climbing a mountain is present". ṭāliʿan has tanwīn and governs jabalan. Mine: لَا رَاغِبًا فِي الخَيْرِ خَائِبٌ, "no one who wants the good is disappointed".

The ḵabar is very often deleted when obvious: لَا بَأْسَ, "no harm (in it)", لَا شَكَّ, "no doubt". لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ: ilāha is ism lā mabnī ʿalā l-fatḥ fī maḥall naṣb; the ḵabar is deleted (taqdīruhu mawjūdun or ḥaqqun); illā is ḥarf istiṯnāʾ mulġā; the divine name is badal marfūʿ — from the ḵabar, or from the maḥall of lā and its ism (Dr. Saeed accepts either on sheets but wants one written in full, not "badal" by itself).

Repeated lā with two nouns joined by wāw: لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ allows five readings (both mabnī; first mabnī second manṣūb with tanwīn; first mabnī second marfūʿ; first marfūʿ second mabnī; both marfūʿ). She does not expect us to list them on the exam, only to accept that the standard reading (both fatḥ) is "built + built".

what are the three kinds of ism after lā al-nāfiya li-l-jins and how is each marked?
?
mufrad: mabnī on what it would be manṣūb with (fatḥ, yāʾ for dual and sound masc. plural, kasr or fatḥ for sound fem. plural), fī maḥall naṣb;
muḍāf: muʿrab, manṣūb, no tanwīn (لَا طَالِبَ عِلْمٍ نَادِمٌ);
šabīh bi-l-muḍāf: muʿrab, manṣūb, with tanwīn (لَا طَالِعًا جَبَلًا حَاضِرٌ) ^nh-la-three
