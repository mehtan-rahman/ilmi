## the frame: nawāsiḵ

Dr. Saeed opened the semester with the word نَاسِخ (nāsiḵ, pl. nawāsiḵ), "abrogator": a word that enters a nominal sentence (جُمْلَة اسْمِيَّة, mubtadaʾ + ḵabar) and cancels (yansaḵu) its original case marking. Without it both parts are marfūʿ. With it, one of them changes. Three families, three different patterns:

| family | what it does to the mubtadaʾ | what it does to the ḵabar | names after it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| kāna wa-aḵawātuhā (verbs) | stays marfūʿ | becomes manṣūb | ism kāna / ḵabar kāna |
| inna wa-aḵawātuhā (particles) | becomes manṣūb | stays marfūʿ | ism inna / ḵabar inna |
| ẓanna wa-aḵawātuhā (verbs of the heart) | becomes manṣūb | becomes manṣūb | mafʿūl bihi awwal / ṯānin |

Plus kāda wa-aḵawātuhā (afʿāl al-muqāraba: kāda, ʿasā, šaraʿa…), which work exactly like kāna except the ḵabar has to be a clause with a muḍāriʿ verb. She will come back to those after the conditionals.

The rule for kāna is one line: it raises the ism and puts the ḵabar in the accusative. Everything else in this unit is the list, the conditions, the meanings, and the exceptions.

الجَوُّ بَارِدٌ ← كَانَ الجَوُّ بَارِدًا

al-jawwu bāridun → kāna l-jawwu bāridan, "the weather is cold" → "the weather was cold".

## why "nāqiṣ"

They are called الأَفْعَال النَّاقِصَة (al-afʿāl al-nāqiṣa), the "incomplete" or "deficient" verbs. Two explanations given in lecture, and Dr. Saeed wanted both:

The first is about the sentence. A complete (tāmm) verb is satisfied by its fāʿil: جَلَسَ الوَلَدُ, "the boy sat", is a full statement. كَانَ الوَلَدُ, "the boy was", is not; it hangs until you say what he was. So the verb needs a second element, the ḵabar, and that makes it deficient.

The second is about meaning. A real verb carries two things: an event (حَدَث, ḥadaṯ) and a time (زَمَن). kāna carries only the time. It says "in the past" and nothing happened. Ibn Hišām and the later grammarians lean on this one; it explains why these verbs have no proper maṣdar in their nāqiṣ use. [she said the "no maṣdar" claim is disputed: some allow كَوْن as the maṣdar of nāqiṣ kāna. ask in OH whether we will be tested on the dispute or just the claim]

The ism of kāna is not a fāʿil. It looks like one (marfūʿ, after a verb) and that is exactly the mistake. In iʿrāb write اسْمُ كَانَ مَرْفُوعٌ.

| word | iʿrāb |
| --- | --- |
| كَانَ | فِعْلٌ مَاضٍ نَاقِصٌ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى الفَتْحِ |
| الجَوُّ | اسْمُ كَانَ مَرْفُوعٌ وَعَلَامَةُ رَفْعِهِ الضَّمَّةُ الظَّاهِرَةُ عَلَى آخِرِهِ |
| بَارِدًا | خَبَرُ كَانَ مَنْصُوبٌ وَعَلَامَةُ نَصْبِهِ الفَتْحَةُ الظَّاهِرَةُ عَلَى آخِرِهِ |

## the meanings, one by one

**kāna.** Past state: كَانَ الطَّالِبُ مَرِيضًا, kāna ṭ-ṭālibu marīḍan, "the student was ill". With a muḍāriʿ in the ḵabar it gives the habitual or progressive past: كَانَ يَدْرُسُ كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ, kāna yadrusu kulla laylatin, "he used to study every night". Dr. Saeed: in the Qurʾān kāna with God as ism (وَكَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَحِيمًا) is not past at all; it means permanence, "God is and always has been". The grammarians call this kāna li-l-istimrār. [not on the exam, she said, but she will "be pleased" if we notice it in a passage]

**ṣāra.** Transformation, the most "verbal" of them: صَارَ المَاءُ ثَلْجًا, ṣāra l-māʾu ṯaljan, "the water became ice". Every other "became" in the list (aṣbaḥa, amsā, aḍḥā) is the time-of-day verb stretched to mean ṣāra; in MSA aṣbaḥa has mostly lost its morning and just means "became": أَصْبَحَ الطَّرِيقُ مُزْدَحِمًا, aṣbaḥa ṭ-ṭarīqu muzdaḥiman, "the road became crowded" (no morning implied).

**the times of day.** aṣbaḥa (ṣabāḥ, morning), aḍḥā (ḍuḥā, the late morning, roughly 9 to noon), ẓalla (the day as a whole, "all day long"), amsā (masāʾ, evening), bāta (the night). Their original sense is "X was Y during that part of the day": أَمْسَى الشَّارِعُ هَادِئًا, amsā š-šāriʿu hādiʾan, "in the evening the street was quiet"; بَاتَ المَرِيضُ قَلِقًا, bāta l-marīḍu qaliqan, "the patient spent the night anxious".

**laysa.** Negates the present by default: لَيْسَ الامْتِحَانُ صَعْبًا, laysa l-imtiḥānu ṣaʿban, "the exam is not hard". With a time word or context it can negate the past or future. It is jāmid: it has no muḍāriʿ, no imperative, no maṣdar. It does conjugate for person in the perfect (lastu, lasta, laysat…).

**mā zāla and its group.** zāla means "to cease", so negated it means "did not cease = still": مَا زَالَ المَطَرُ نَازِلًا, mā zāla l-maṭaru nāzilan, "the rain is still falling" (lit. "did not cease falling"). لَا يَزَالُ أَخِي مَرِيضًا, lā yazālu aḵī marīḍan, "my brother is still ill". Two different verbs hide under the spelling زَالَ: zāla yazālu (faʿila pattern, nāqiṣ, the one we want) and zāla yazūlu, "to vanish, go away", which is tāmm and has a fāʿil: زَالَ الخَطَرُ, "the danger passed". The imperfect tells them apart. The perfect does not.

**mā dāma.** "As long as". The mā turns dāma and what follows into a maṣdar of time: سَأَنْتَظِرُكَ مَا دَامَ البَابُ مَفْتُوحًا, sa-antaẓiruka mā dāma l-bābu maftūḥan, "I will wait for you as long as the door is open" = for the duration of the door's being open. Because it is a time clause it cannot open a sentence; it hangs off a main clause. Dr. Saeed: "mā dāma never starts anything."

in كَانَ يَدْرُسُ كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ, kāna with a muḍāriʿ in the ḵabar expresses {{c1::the habitual (or continuous) past, "he used to study"}} ^nh-kana-hab

## conjugating them (where ṣarf comes back)

kāna, ṣāra, bāta, dāma, zāla are hollow; aḍḥā and amsā are defective; ẓalla and infakka are doubled; fatiʾa is hamzated. So the whole of the ARAB 302 weak-verbs note comes back here (re-reading that note now, it is on a read schedule).

kāna (k-w-n, hollow with wāw, faʿala/yafʿulu):

| person | perfect | imperfect | jussive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| anā | كُنْتُ kuntu | أَكُونُ akūnu | لَمْ أَكُنْ lam akun |
| anta | كُنْتَ kunta | تَكُونُ takūnu | لَمْ تَكُنْ lam takun |
| anti | كُنْتِ kunti | تَكُونِينَ takūnīna | لَمْ تَكُونِي lam takūnī |
| huwa | كَانَ kāna | يَكُونُ yakūnu | لَمْ يَكُنْ lam yakun |
| hiya | كَانَتْ kānat | تَكُونُ takūnu | لَمْ تَكُنْ lam takun |
| naḥnu | كُنَّا kunnā | نَكُونُ nakūnu | لَمْ نَكُنْ lam nakun |
| antum | كُنْتُمْ kuntum | تَكُونُونَ takūnūna | لَمْ تَكُونُوا lam takūnū |
| hum | كَانُوا kānū | يَكُونُونَ yakūnūna | لَمْ يَكُونُوا lam yakūnū |
| hunna | كُنَّ kunna | يَكُنَّ yakunna | لَمْ يَكُنَّ lam yakunna |

The vowel of the first radical in the perfect with consonant-initial endings (kuntu) is the ḍamma of the wāw-hollow type, versus biʿtu for yāʾ-hollow and ṣirtu for ṣāra (ṣ-y-r). The long vowel drops before a sukūn: yakūnu → lam yakun.

The jussive of kāna can also drop its nūn: lam yaku for lam yakun. Allowed only when the next word does not start with a sukūn (i.e. not before the article) and the verb is not attached to a pronoun. The Qurʾānic example from lecture: وَلَمْ أَكُ بَغِيًّا (Maryam 19:20), wa-lam aku baġiyyan, "nor was I unchaste". Here baġiyyan is ḵabar of akun, manṣūb.

ẓalla, doubled: ẓalaltu is the full form of the perfect with a consonant ending, but the classical language also has ẓaltu and ẓiltu, dropping one lām. فَظَلْتُمْ تَفَكَّهُونَ (al-Wāqiʿa 56:65), fa-ẓaltum tafakkahūna, "you would remain marveling". Note the ḵabar there is a verbal sentence, so no visible accusative.

laysa: لَسْتُ lastu, لَسْتَ lasta, لَسْتِ lasti, لَيْسَ laysa, لَيْسَتْ laysat, لَسْنَا lasnā, لَسْتُمْ lastum, لَيْسُوا laysū, لَسْنَ lasna. Short a in the stem whenever the ending starts with a consonant: same mechanics as a hollow verb, which is one of the arguments that laysa was originally a verb of the faʿila type, lays- from layisa. [Dr. Saeed's aside, not in book]

the jussive لَمْ يَكُنْ may shorten to {{c1::لَمْ يَكُ}} when the next word does not begin with a sukūn, as in وَلَمْ أَكُ بَغِيًّا ^nh-lam-yaku

## the ḵabar: what shapes it takes

The ḵabar of kāna can be anything a ḵabar of a nominal sentence can be:

A single word (mufrad), in which case the case ending is visible: كَانَ الطَّالِبُ مَرِيضًا.

A verbal sentence: كَانَ الطَّالِبُ يَكْتُبُ الدَّرْسَ. Here يَكْتُبُ الدَّرْسَ is jumla fiʿliyya fī maḥall naṣb ḵabar kāna. The verb inside has its own hidden fāʿil (huwa) referring back to al-ṭālib, which is the rābiṭ (linker) the sentence-ḵabar requires.

A nominal sentence: كَانَ الطَّالِبُ أَبُوهُ طَبِيبٌ, "the student's father was a doctor" (lit. "the student was, his father a doctor"). أَبُوهُ طَبِيبٌ is jumla ismiyya fī maḥall naṣb; the -hu is the rābiṭ. Inside it, abūhu is mubtadaʾ marfūʿ by the wāw (one of the five nouns) and ṭabībun is its ḵabar.

A shibh jumla (prepositional phrase or ẓarf): كَانَ الكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ. Strictly the shibh jumla is mutaʿalliq with a deleted ḵabar (kāʾinan / istaqarra); Dr. Saeed accepts "شِبْهُ الجُمْلَةِ فِي مَحَلِّ نَصْبٍ خَبَرُ كَانَ" on sheets, which is what the textbook writes too.

## order: when the ḵabar comes first

Normal order is kāna – ism – ḵabar. The ḵabar may move in between (tawassuṭ al-ḵabar): كَانَ مَرِيضًا الطَّالِبُ is grammatical (unusual in prose, common in poetry).

The ḵabar must come before the ism when the ism is indefinite and the ḵabar is a shibh jumla, exactly as in a plain nominal sentence (فِي الصَّفِّ طَالِبٌ, not طَالِبٌ فِي الصَّفِّ): كَانَ فِي الصَّفِّ طَالِبٌ جَدِيدٌ, kāna fī ṣ-ṣaffi ṭālibun jadīdun, "there was a new student in the class". The newspaper "there was / there were" is this construction. ṭālibun here is ism kāna muʾaḵḵar, marfūʿ; fī ṣ-ṣaffi is the ḵabar muqaddam.

The ḵabar can even precede kāna itself (مَرِيضًا كَانَ الطَّالِبُ) except with laysa (disputed) and the verbs that need mā, because mā (negative or maṣdariyya) takes the head of its clause. [the laysa dispute: Baṣrans allow, some Kūfans don't? I wrote "disputed" and an arrow. check the book §9.4]

## laysa and the bāʾ al-zāʾida

The ḵabar of laysa very often takes a "redundant" bāʾ: لَسْتُ بِغَافِلٍ عَنْ هَذَا, lastu bi-ġāfilin ʿan hāḏā, "I am not unaware of this". The bāʾ adds emphasis (tawkīd al-nafy). It is a ḥarf jarr zāʾid: it changes the visible case to genitive but the word keeps its grammatical function. So ġāfilin is ḵabar laysa, majrūr lafẓan, manṣūb maḥallan. The standard formula:

بِغَافِلٍ: البَاءُ حَرْفُ جَرٍّ زَائِدٌ، وَغَافِلٍ خَبَرُ لَيْسَ مَجْرُورٌ لَفْظًا مَنْصُوبٌ مَحَلًّا.

The same bāʾ shows up with the ḵabar of mā al-ḥijāziyya (mā that works like laysa: مَا هَذَا بَشَرًا, Yūsuf 12:31) — which is a separate topic, next week's handout. Do not confuse: there the ḵabar of mā is manṣūb in the Ḥijāzī dialect and marfūʿ in Tamīmī. [I wrote "ḵabar of mā manṣūb in Ḥijāzī, marfūʿ in Tamīmī" and underlined it twice]

## kāna tāmma, and kāna zāʾida

Most of the group can also be tāmm (complete) verbs, with a fāʿil and no ḵabar. Then they are not nawāsiḵ at all.

kāna tāmma means "to exist, happen, take place". Lecture example: وَإِنْ كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍ فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَى مَيْسَرَةٍ (al-Baqara 2:280), "and if there is someone in hardship, then (let there be) a delay until ease". ḏū ʿusratin is fāʿil of kāna, marfūʿ by the wāw (five nouns). Nothing manṣūb follows; the sentence is complete.

aṣbaḥa tāmma, "to enter the morning": the morning supplication أَصْبَحْنَا وَأَصْبَحَ المُلْكُ لِلَّهِ, aṣbaḥnā wa-aṣbaḥa l-mulku li-llāhi, "we have entered the morning, and the dominion has entered the morning belonging to God". The first aṣbaḥnā is tāmm (the nā is fāʿil); the second aṣbaḥa is nāqiṣ, al-mulku its ism and li-llāhi a shibh jumla in the place of its ḵabar. Dr. Saeed loves this example: same verb, both uses, four words apart.

kāna zāʾida (redundant): between mā and the verb of wonder, مَا كَانَ أَجْمَلَ السَّمَاءَ!, mā kāna ajmala s-samāʾa!, "how beautiful the sky was!". kāna here has no ism and no ḵabar; it only adds the past. Parse: فِعْلٌ مَاضٍ زَائِدٌ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى الفَتْحِ لَا عَمَلَ لَهُ.

Deletion of kāna with its ism after in and law, leaving the ḵabar: اِلْتَمِسْ وَلَوْ خَاتَمًا مِنْ حَدِيدٍ (ḥadīṯ), "seek, even if (it be) a ring of iron". ḵātaman is ḵabar of a deleted kāna: wa-law kāna l-multamasu ḵātaman. That is why it is manṣūb with no visible governor. [this one tripped half the class]

## case endings that are not visible

The ḵabar is manṣūb, but the sign of naṣb changes with the noun's type. This is where the sheets lose points.

Sound masculine plural, with yāʾ: كَانَ المُعَلِّمُونَ حَاضِرِينَ, kāna l-muʿallimūna ḥāḍirīna. ism marfūʿ with wāw, ḵabar manṣūb with yāʾ.

Dual, with yāʾ: كَانَ الطَّالِبَانِ مُتْعَبَيْنِ, kāna ṭ-ṭālibāni mutʿabayni.

Sound feminine plural, with kasra: لَيْسَتِ الطَّالِبَاتُ غَائِبَاتٍ, laysati ṭ-ṭālibātu ġāʾibātin. The ḵabar takes kasra in naṣb (al-jamʿ al-muʾannaṯ al-sālim yunṣabu bi-l-kasra nāʾibatan ʿan al-fatḥa).

The five nouns, with alif: صَارَ زَيْدٌ أَخَاكَ فِي السَّكَنِ, "Zayd became your brother in the dorm" (made-up, she accepted it). aḵāka manṣūb with alif.

Defective noun (manqūṣ, ends in -ī): the fatḥa is visible, the ḍamma and kasra are not. أَصْبَحَ أَخِي قَاضِيًا, aṣbaḥa aḵī qāḍiyan, fatḥa ẓāhira on the yāʾ. But كَانَ القَاضِي عَادِلًا, kāna l-qāḍī ʿādilan: al-qāḍī is ism kāna marfūʿ bi-ḍamma muqaddara ʿalā l-yāʾ li-l-ṯiqal (heaviness).

Noun ending in alif (maqṣūr): no ending is visible at all. كَانَ الفَتَى مَرِيضًا, kāna l-fatā marīḍan: al-fatā is ism kāna marfūʿ bi-ḍamma muqaddara ʿalā l-alif li-l-taʿaḏḏur (impossibility). And aḵī in aṣbaḥa aḵī: marfūʿ bi-ḍamma muqaddara, prevented by the kasra the yāʾ of the first person demands (li-štiġāl al-maḥall bi-ḥarakat al-munāsaba). [three different reasons for muqaddara: taʿaḏḏur on alif, ṯiqal on yāʾ, munāsaba before -ī. the third is the one I forget]

## worked iʿrāb (from sheet 1, corrected)

لَا يَزَالُ الطِّفْلُ نَائِمًا — lā yazālu ṭ-ṭiflu nāʾiman, "the child is still asleep".

- لَا: ḥarf nafy mabnī ʿalā l-sukūn, lā maḥall lahu min al-iʿrāb.
- يَزَالُ: fiʿl muḍāriʿ nāqiṣ marfūʿ bi-l-ḍamma.
- الطِّفْلُ: ism lā yazālu marfūʿ bi-l-ḍamma.
- نَائِمًا: ḵabar lā yazālu manṣūb bi-l-fatḥa.

ظَلَّ الطُّلَّابُ يُرَاجِعُونَ حَتَّى الفَجْرِ — ẓalla ṭ-ṭullābu yurājiʿūna ḥattā l-fajri, "the students kept revising until dawn".

- ظَلَّ: fiʿl māḍin nāqiṣ mabnī ʿalā l-fatḥ.
- الطُّلَّابُ: ism ẓalla marfūʿ.
- يُرَاجِعُونَ: fiʿl muḍāriʿ marfūʿ bi-ṯubūt al-nūn (one of the five verbs), wāw al-jamāʿa fāʿil; the jumla fiʿliyya fī maḥall naṣb ḵabar ẓalla.
- حَتَّى الفَجْرِ: ḥarf jarr + ism majrūr; mutaʿalliq bi-yurājiʿūna.

سَأَبْقَى فِي المَكْتَبَةِ مَا دُمْتُ قَادِرًا عَلَى التَّرْكِيزِ — sa-abqā fī l-maktabati mā dumtu qādiran ʿalā t-tarkīzi, "I'll stay in the library as long as I can concentrate".

- مَا: maṣdariyya ẓarfiyya.
- دُمْتُ: fiʿl māḍin nāqiṣ mabnī ʿalā l-sukūn li-ttiṣālihi bi-tāʾ al-fāʿil; the tāʾ is ism dāma, mabnī ʿalā l-ḍamm fī maḥall rafʿ. [careful: it is still called tāʾ al-fāʿil as a name for the morpheme even though it is the ism here. Dr. Saeed: "the name of the morpheme does not tell you its function"]
- قَادِرًا: ḵabar dāma manṣūb.
- mā + dumtu… = maṣdar muʾawwal (muddata dawāmī qādiran) fī maḥall naṣb ẓarf zamān.

in مَا دُمْتُ قَادِرًا, the tāʾ of dumtu is {{c1::ism dāma (mabnī ʿalā l-ḍamm fī maḥall rafʿ)}}, even though the morpheme is called tāʾ al-fāʿil ^nh-dumtu

## things to watch on sheets

When you see a time-of-day verb, first ask whether it is nāqiṣ (there is a manṣūb predicate) or tāmm (complete without one). أَمْسَيْنَا, "we entered the evening", alone, is tāmm.

When the ḵabar is a sentence, write "fī maḥall naṣb" and name the rābiṭ.

When kāna appears with no manṣūb anywhere, check for kāna tāmma, kāna zāʾida, or the ḵabar being a sentence or shibh jumla before panicking.
