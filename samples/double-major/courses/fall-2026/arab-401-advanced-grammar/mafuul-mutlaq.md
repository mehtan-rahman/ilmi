## definition

المَفْعُولُ المُطْلَقُ: مَصْدَرٌ مَنْصُوبٌ يُذْكَرُ بَعْدَ فِعْلٍ مِنْ لَفْظِهِ تَوْكِيدًا لَهُ أَوْ بَيَانًا لِنَوْعِهِ أَوْ عَدَدِهِ.

"The absolute object is a maṣdar, manṣūb, mentioned after a verb of its own root, to emphasize it, or to show its kind, or its number." The "absolute" (muṭlaq) because unlike the other mafʿūls it is not restricted by a preposition: mafʿūl bihi = done *to* it, mafʿūl fīhi = done *in* it, mafʿūl lahu = done *for* it, mafʿūl maʿahu = done *with* it, the mafʿūl muṭlaq is just "the doing" itself. Sībawayh's view, per lecture, is that it is the real object of every verb, since every verb's event is its maṣdar. [that's why the fāʿil "does" it: ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ ضَرْبًا, the thing Zayd did was a beating]

## the three functions

**tawkīd (emphasis)**: the maṣdar alone, no description, no number. فَرِحْتُ فَرَحًا, fariḥtu faraḥan, "I was really glad". It adds nothing but force. The Qurʾānic example she wrote: وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًا (al-Nisāʾ 4:164), "and God spoke to Moses directly" — the maṣdar of Form II kallama, tafʿīl. The tawkīd here is theological: the speech was real speech, not metaphor. [she said the commentators use this verse in exactly that argument; not our business, but it shows why the grammar matters]

**bayān al-nawʿ (showing the kind)**: the maṣdar is qualified, by an adjective or by iḍāfa.

سَارَ الجُنْدِيُّ سَيْرًا سَرِيعًا, sāra l-jundiyyu sayran sarīʿan, "the soldier marched quickly" (a quick marching). sarīʿan is naʿt of sayran, manṣūb.

سَارَ الجُنْدِيُّ سَيْرَ الأَبْطَالِ, sāra l-jundiyyu sayra l-abṭāli, "the soldier marched as heroes march". sayra is mafʿūl muṭlaq, muḍāf; al-abṭāli muḍāf ilayhi majrūr.

**bayān al-ʿadad (showing the number)**: the nomen vicis (ism al-marra) or a number.

قَفَزَ الوَلَدُ قَفْزَتَيْنِ, qafaza l-waladu qafzatayni, "the boy jumped twice". qafzatayni is a dual, manṣūb with yāʾ.

دَقَّ الجَرَسُ ثَلَاثَ دَقَّاتٍ, daqqa l-jarasu ṯalāṯa daqqātin, "the bell rang three times". ṯalāṯa is nāʾib ʿan al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq (the number stands in for it), daqqātin muḍāf ilayhi.

سَارَ الجُنْدِيُّ {{c1::سَيْرًا سَرِيعًا}} — mafʿūl muṭlaq for showing the kind (bayān al-nawʿ) ^nh-mm-sayran

## what can stand in for the maṣdar (nāʾib ʿan al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq)

When the maṣdar itself is not there, something else can take its place and take its case. Dr. Saeed's list (the textbook's is longer):

كُلّ and بَعْض, added to the maṣdar: اجْتَهَدْتُ كُلَّ الاجْتِهَادِ, ijtahadtu kulla l-ijtihādi, "I made every effort"; فَهِمْتُ بَعْضَ الفَهْمِ, "I understood somewhat". kulla is nāʾib ʿan al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq, manṣūb; the maṣdar is muḍāf ilayhi.

A synonymous maṣdar (murādif): قَعَدْتُ جُلُوسًا, qaʿadtu julūsan, "I sat down (a sitting)". julūs is not from q-ʿ-d but means the same.

A maṣdar of a different form of the same root: تَبَتَّلْ إِلَيْهِ تَبْتِيلًا (al-Muzzammil 73:8), "devote yourself to Him with devotion": verb Form V, maṣdar Form II. [first time I've seen this; she said it is the standard example for maṣdar muḵālif]

A number: ثَلَاثَ دَقَّاتٍ (above).

The instrument: ضَرَبْتُهُ سَوْطًا, "I struck him with a whip" (a whip's blow).

A demonstrative followed by the maṣdar: قُلْتُ ذَلِكَ القَوْلَ.

An adjective of the deleted maṣdar: سِرْتُ طَوِيلًا, "I walked a long time" (= sayran ṭawīlan). [she said "careful, some parse this as ẓarf". the book says nāʾib ʿan al-maṣdar]

in اجْتَهَدْتُ كُلَّ الاجْتِهَادِ, kulla is {{c1::nāʾib ʿan al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq}} and al-ijtihādi is muḍāf ilayhi ^nh-mm-kulla

## with the verb deleted

Some maṣdars are used without their verb so often that the verb is never said. These are fixed expressions, and they are all mafʿūl muṭlaq of a deleted verb:

شُكْرًا, šukran, "thanks" (= aškuruka šukran).
عَفْوًا, ʿafwan, "pardon; you're welcome" (= aʿfū ʿafwan).
سَمْعًا وَطَاعَةً, samʿan wa-ṭāʿatan, "hearing and obeying" (= asmaʿu samʿan wa-uṭīʿu ṭāʿatan).
صَبْرًا, ṣabran, "patience!" (= iṣbir ṣabran).
حَقًّا, ḥaqqan, "truly".

## the maṣdar that is not mafʿūl muṭlaq

The test is not "is it a manṣūb maṣdar". It is "is it the maṣdar of *this* verb (or something standing in for it)?" If the maṣdar is the object of a different verb it is a mafʿūl bihi: أَحْبَبْتُ القِرَاءَةَ, aḥbabtu l-qirāʾata, "I loved reading". qirāʾa is not the doing of aḥbabtu; it is the thing loved. mafʿūl bihi.

And a manṣūb maṣdar of purpose is mafʿūl lahu: قُمْتُ احْتِرَامًا لِلْمُعَلِّمِ, "I stood up out of respect for the teacher" (why did you stand? out of respect). [next week's topic, she said, so here only to mark the border]

no: it is a maṣdar, but of a different verb from aḥbabtu, and it is the thing loved, so it is mafʿūl bihi ^nh-mm-qiraa
