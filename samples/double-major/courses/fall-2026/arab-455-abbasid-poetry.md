## the seminar

ARAB 455, Seminar: Poetry of the ʿAbbāsid Age. Prof. Haddad. Thursdays, three hours with a break. Twelve of us, mostly Arabic majors, two from comp lit, one grad student auditing (Yusuf) who has read all of al-Mutanabbī's dīwān and lets you know.

From the syllabus, roughly: the course reads the Arabic qaṣīda from its pre-Islamic form through its transformation under the ʿAbbāsids (132/750 onward), with the muḥdathūn ("moderns") and the critics who argued about them. All readings in Arabic; translations allowed for orientation but "the paper is about the Arabic or it is about a translator". Each week: 60–120 lines of verse prepared for close reading, plus one piece of secondary reading. Prof. Haddad calls on people to read aloud and scan. Come with the meter identified.

Grading: participation and weekly scansion 25%, one presentation 20%, term paper 55%. No exams. (The only course this term with no midterm, thank god.)

## my notes, how they work

Handwritten in the session in the green notebook (Arabic on the right-hand pages, English on the left, because it is less miserable that way), typed up here within a few days. Each session's topic is a tab. Cards go inline where the fact is. Verse I want by heart goes in as a cloze on the second hemistich.

The term paper lives in its own tab with its outline and reading notes under it. The clipped article on translation is under the seminar too, since Prof. Haddad sent it round.
