The address to two companions is a small case of the whole problem. The verb is dual: the poet speaks to two people. The convention lasted long after anyone imagined real companions at the ruins. English has no dual. Yet the dual matters: it makes the prelude a scene with witnesses rather than a private lament. Lose the two and you lose the first audience inside the poem, the one that stands for us.
