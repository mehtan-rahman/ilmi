Typed up 09-19. This one is mostly for drilling. Scansion is a quarter of the grade and she does it cold at the start of every session ("what meter, Mr./Ms. X?"), so the patterns have to be automatic.

Notation in these notes: – long syllable, ∪ short, × anceps (either). Hemistichs separated by ‖. Feet separated by |.

## al-Ḫalīl and the system

Prosody (ʿarūḍ, عَرُوض) as a science is credited to al-Ḫalīl b. Aḥmad al-Farāhīdī of Basra (d. c. 175/791, some sources 170/786), also the author of the first Arabic dictionary (*Kitāb al-ʿAyn*, which he did not finish). The story: he heard the rhythm of a coppersmith's hammers in the market. Prof. Haddad: "the story is false and correct: the system *is* a theory of beats."

He described **15 meters** (buḥūr, sg. baḥr, بَحْر "sea"); al-Aḫfaš al-Awsaṭ (d. c. 215/830) added a 16th, the mutadārik. The meters are organized into **five circles** (dawāʾir): rotate the sequence of a circle's units from a different starting point and you get the other meters of the same circle. The ṭawīl, madīd and basīṭ share a circle.

## the building blocks

Al-Ḫalīl's units are counted in letters, mutaḥarrik (voweled) and sākin (unvoweled), not in syllables, but syllables are easier and give the same result, so that is how she teaches it:

- **sabab ḫafīf** (light cord): voweled + unvoweled = one long syllable, e.g. *lan*, *mā* → –
- **sabab ṯaqīl** (heavy cord): two voweled = two shorts, e.g. *laka* (as la-ka) → ∪ ∪
- **watad majmūʿ** (joined peg): two voweled + unvoweled → ∪ –, e.g. *ʿalā*
- **watad mafrūq** (separated peg): voweled, unvoweled, voweled → – ∪, e.g. *qāla* (before a following consonant)

The pegs (awtād) are stable; the cords (asbāb) take the variations (zihāfāt). That is the single most useful fact for scanning: if a foot looks wrong, the peg is where you anchor it.

the units of al-Ḫalīl's feet: a watad majmūʿ is {{c1::∪ –}}, a sabab ḫafīf is {{c2::–}}, a sabab ṯaqīl is {{c3::∪ ∪}} ^ab-sabab-watad

Syllable rules for scanning (reminders to self, since I keep getting these wrong):

- scan what is pronounced, not what is written: hamzat al-waṣl drops (wa-l-laylu → wal-lay-lu), assimilated lām counts once doubled (aš-šuʿarāʾ → aš-šu-), shadda = two consonants (the first closes the syllable before).
- tanwīn is a consonant: ḥabībin = ḥa-bī-bin, closed final.
- the last syllable of a hemistich is always counted long (anceps resolved long), and at line-end the rhyme vowel is lengthened (iṭlāq): manzili → man-zi-lī.
- pronominal suffix -hu after a short vowel is often lengthened (-hū) when the meter wants it; same for -hi. ʿarūḍ allows this (išbāʿ).

## the feet (taf ʿīlāt)

The feet are named by nonsense-words from the root f-ʿ-l, the same way morphology uses faʿala:

| foot | Arabic | pattern | built from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| faʿūlun | فَعُولُنْ | ∪ – – | watad majmūʿ + sabab |
| mafāʿīlun | مَفَاعِيلُنْ | ∪ – – – | watad + 2 sabab |
| fāʿilun | فَاعِلُنْ | – ∪ – | sabab + watad |
| mustafʿilun | مُسْتَفْعِلُنْ | – – ∪ – | 2 sabab + watad |
| fāʿilātun | فَاعِلَاتُنْ | – ∪ – – | sabab + watad + sabab |
| mutafāʿilun | مُتَفَاعِلُنْ | ∪ ∪ – ∪ – | sabab ṯaqīl + sabab + watad |
| mufāʿalatun | مُفَاعَلَتُنْ | ∪ – ∪ ∪ – | watad + sabab ṯaqīl + sabab |

## the four meters for this course

About 80% of what we read is in these four. ṭawīl alone is something like a third of classical poetry (Prof. Haddad's number, from somebody's count; she said "roughly a third, don't quote me").

### ṭawīl (الطَّوِيل, "the long")

Per hemistich: faʿūlun mafāʿīlun faʿūlun mafāʿilun

∪ – × | ∪ – × – | ∪ – × | ∪ – ∪ –

The last foot of each hemistich is almost always mafāʿilun (∪ – ∪ –), i.e. with **qabḍ** (dropping the fifth letter, the quiescent: mafāʿīlun → mafāʿilun). The third syllable of faʿūlun can be shortened too (faʿūlu, ∪ – ∪). The dominant meter of the jāhilī ode and of grave subjects. The Imruʾ al-Qays first line scans:

qi-fā-nab | ki-min-ḏik-rā | ḥa-bī-bin | wa-man-zi-lī
∪ – – | ∪ – – – | ∪ – – | ∪ – ∪ –

### basīṭ (البَسِيط, "the outspread")

Per hemistich: mustafʿilun fāʿilun mustafʿilun faʿilun

× – ∪ – | × ∪ – | × – ∪ – | ∪ ∪ – (or – –)

The last foot is usually faʿilun (∪ ∪ –, ḫabn of fāʿilun) or faʿlun (– –). Feel: a long swing then a short tumble at the end of each half. Al-Mutanabbī's "al-ḫaylu wa-l-laylu" line and Abū Nuwās's "daʿ ʿanka lawmī" are basīṭ. Abū Tammām's Amorium ode too.

al-ḫay-lu-wal | lay-lu-wal | bay-dā-ʾu-taʿ | ri-fu-nī
– – ∪ – | – ∪ – | – – ∪ – | ∪ ∪ –

### kāmil (الكَامِل, "the complete")

Per hemistich: mutafāʿilun mutafāʿilun mutafāʿilun

∪ ∪ – ∪ – (×3)

The common variation is **iḍmār**: the second letter quiescent, mutafāʿilun → mutfāʿilun (– – ∪ –). And mutfāʿilun has exactly the shape of mustafʿilun, so a kāmil line with iḍmār in every foot looks like rajaz (mustafʿilun ×3). You tell them apart by finding one foot with the two shorts. ʿAntara's muʿallaqa is kāmil:

> هَلْ غَادَرَ الشُّعَرَاءُ مِنْ مُتَرَدَّمِ / أَمْ هَلْ عَرَفْتَ الدَّارَ بَعْدَ تَوَهُّمِ

"Have the poets left any spot unpatched? / Or did you know the abode, after doubting?" (mutaraddam: a place in a garment needing a patch, so: have the poets left anything unsaid; my gloss, the commentators disagree.)

hal-ġā-da-raš | šu-ʿa-rā-ʾu-min | mu-ta-rad-da-mī
– – ∪ – | ∪ ∪ – ∪ – | ∪ ∪ – ∪ –

First foot with iḍmār, then two clean ones.

the kāmil, per hemistich: {{c1::mutafāʿilun ×3}} (∪ ∪ – ∪ –); with iḍmār a foot becomes – – ∪ –, the shape of {{c2::mustafʿilun (so it can look like rajaz)}} ^ab-kamil

### wāfir (الوَافِر, "the abundant")

Per hemistich: mufāʿalatun mufāʿalatun faʿūlun

∪ – ∪ ∪ – | ∪ – ∪ ∪ – | ∪ – –

The variation is **ʿaṣb**: the fifth letter quiescent, mufāʿalatun → mufāʿaltun (∪ – – –, the shape of mafāʿīlun). The last foot is always faʿūlun (it is really a curtailed mufāʿalatun, the qaṭf). Good example, al-Mutanabbī:

> إِذَا غَامَرْتَ فِي شَرَفٍ مَرُومِ / فَلَا تَقْنَعْ بِمَا دُونَ النُّجُومِ

"When you hazard yourself for an honour that is striven after / do not be content with anything below the stars."

i-ḏā-ġā-mar | ta-fī-ša-ra-fin | ma-rū-mī
∪ – – – | ∪ – ∪ ∪ – | ∪ – –

### ramal, for Abū Nuwās

Not one of the four but it keeps coming up. Per hemistich: fāʿilātun fāʿilātun fāʿilātun, the last usually curtailed to fāʿilun (ḥaḏf). Light, lilting; much used by the muḥdathūn.

## other terms from the handout

- **ʿarūḍ** is also the name of the last foot of the first hemistich; **ḍarb** the last foot of the second. Confusing: the whole science and one foot have the same name.
- **ḥašw**: the feet in between.
- **zihāf** (pl. zihāfāt): an optional variation in a cord, anywhere in the line, not binding on the rest of the poem.
- **ʿilla** (pl. ʿilal): a change at the ʿarūḍ or ḍarb that, once used, holds for the whole poem (e.g. ḥaḏf in the ramal).
- **majzūʾ**: a meter with one foot cut from each hemistich (majzūʾ al-kāmil, etc.).
