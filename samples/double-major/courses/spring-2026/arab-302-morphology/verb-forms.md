## the system

Every triliteral root can in principle appear in ten common patterns (أَوْزَان, awzān), numbered I–X by Western grammars. Arabic grammars name them by their perfect pattern with ف ع ل as dummy radicals (فَعَّلَ, أَفْعَلَ…) and call I المُجَرَّد (bare) and II–X المَزِيد (augmented, "with added letters"). Prof. Haddad: use both names on exams. Real roots use only some forms; no root has all ten in living use. Forms XI–XV exist and you will never see them.

The added letters are only these ten: سَأَلْتُمُونِيهَا (the mnemonic word: s ʾ l t m w n y h ā). Every augment in II–X is one of them, or a doubled radical.

the letters of augmentation are summed up in the mnemonic word {{c1::سَأَلْتُمُونِيهَا}} ^sf-saltumuniha

Things the table hides that the cards below test:

The imperfect prefix takes ḍamma in II, III, IV (yu-) and fatḥa everywhere else (ya-). Reason: II, III, IV have four letters in the perfect (fa-ʿ-ʿ-la counts the doubled radical, ʾa-f-ʿa-la counts the hamza); rubāʿī verbs take yu-.

Participles: always mu- + the imperfect stem; active has kasra before the last radical, passive fatḥa. So once you know the imperfect, the participles are free: yu-darris-u → mu-darris (teacher), mu-darras (taught).

The hamza of IV is hamzat qaṭʿ (always pronounced), the alif of VII–X is hamzat waṣl (drops in connected speech). aḵraja vs wa-ntaẓara.

## form I

Three vowel types in the perfect, and the imperfect stem vowel mostly follows from it:

faʿala → yafʿulu (كَتَبَ يَكْتُبُ), yafʿilu (جَلَسَ يَجْلِسُ), or yafʿalu when the second or third radical is a guttural (فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ, ذَهَبَ يَذْهَبُ). Unpredictable between u and i: learn each verb.

faʿila → yafʿalu (عَلِمَ يَعْلَمُ, شَرِبَ يَشْرَبُ). Often states and feelings. A few faʿila/yafʿilu (حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ, also yaḥsabu; وَرِثَ يَرِثُ).

faʿula → yafʿulu (كَبُرَ يَكْبُرُ, حَسُنَ يَحْسُنُ). Always qualities, always intransitive. No passive.

The Form I maṣdar has no rule (see the maṣdar note for the tendencies). Active participle always فَاعِل, passive always مَفْعُول.

faʿula verbs (كَبُرَ، حَسُنَ) have the imperfect {{c1::yafʿulu}} and are always {{c2::intransitive qualities}} ^sf-faula

## form II: فَعَّلَ

Doubling the middle radical "strengthens" the verb. Four uses, in order of frequency in MSA:

Causative (often of an intransitive Form I): عَلِمَ, "to know" → عَلَّمَ, ʿallama, "to teach" (make know). فَرِحَ → فَرَّحَ, "to gladden".

Intensive or repeated: كَسَرَ, "to break" → كَسَّرَ, kassara, "to smash to bits". قَطَعَ → قَطَّعَ, "to cut up".

Denominative (from a noun): خَيْمَة, "tent" → خَيَّمَ, ḵayyama, "to pitch camp". صَوْت → صَوَّتَ, "to vote".

Declarative ("to consider/call someone X"): كَذَّبَ, kaḏḏaba, "to call a liar".

maṣdar تَفْعِيل: تَعْلِيم, تَكْسِير. For defective roots تَفْعِلَة: سَمَّى → تَسْمِيَة, رَبَّى → تَرْبِيَة.

II and IV can both be causative and sometimes both exist with a nuance: أَعْلَمَ, "to inform" vs عَلَّمَ, "to teach". [Haddad: "IV is a single act, II a process". good enough as a rule of thumb, not a law]

the maṣdar of Form II is {{c1::تَفْعِيل}}, and for defective roots {{c2::تَفْعِلَة}} (سَمَّى → تَسْمِيَة) ^sf-ii-masdar

## form III: فَاعَلَ

Lengthening the first vowel: the action is directed at another person, who is the object. Often "to try to do X to someone", or doing it with them.

كَتَبَ, "to write" → كَاتَبَ, kātaba, "to correspond with". قَتَلَ, "to kill" → قَاتَلَ, qātala, "to fight (try to kill)". جَلَسَ → جَالَسَ, "to sit with, keep company". سَبَقَ → سَابَقَ, "to race (someone)".

Two maṣdars: مُفَاعَلَة (مُكَاتَبَة) and فِعَال (قِتَال, جِدَال, نِضَال). Many verbs have both, often with a split in meaning: مُقَاتَلَة (the act of fighting someone) vs قِتَال (fighting, combat). Verbs with initial yāʾ use only مُفَاعَلَة.

The Form VI of the same root makes it mutual: كَاتَبَ زَيْدًا, "he corresponded with Zayd" → تَكَاتَبَا, "they corresponded with each other".

## form IV: أَفْعَلَ

Causative, the main way to make an intransitive verb transitive: خَرَجَ, "to go out" → أَخْرَجَ, aḵraja, "to bring out, take out; (also) to direct a film". جَلَسَ → أَجْلَسَ, "to seat". Also "entering a time or place" (أَصْبَحَ, "to enter the morning", which is why it is in ARAB 401's kāna list) and denominatives of producing (أَثْمَرَ, "to bear fruit").

maṣdar إِفْعَال: إِخْرَاج, إِسْلَام. Hollow roots: إِفَالَة (أَقَامَ → إِقَامَة). Defective: إِفْعَاء (أَعْطَى → إِعْطَاء).

## form V: تَفَعَّلَ

The reflexive or resultative of II: what II does to something, V says happened to it, or what you did to yourself. عَلَّمَ, "to teach" → تَعَلَّمَ, taʿallama, "to learn (teach oneself)". كَسَّرَ → تَكَسَّرَ, "to be shattered". خَرَّجَ, "to graduate (a student)" → تَخَرَّجَ, "to graduate".

Also "making oneself X, affecting a quality": تَشَجَّعَ, "to pluck up courage"; تَكَبَّرَ, "to be arrogant (make oneself big)".

maṣdar تَفَعُّل with ḍamma on the doubled radical: تَعَلُّم. Defective: تَفَعٍّ (تَمَنَّى → تَمَنٍّ, al-tamannī). [the tanwīn kasra on تَمَنٍّ: indefinite manqūṣ. took me three problem sets]

In the imperfect, the tā of V and VI can be dropped in the 2nd person: تَتَذَكَّرُ → تَذَكَّرُ (Qurʾānic style, common in verse).

the Form V maṣdar has {{c1::ḍamma}} on the doubled middle radical (تَعَلُّم), not kasra ^sf-v-masdar

## form VI: تَفَاعَلَ

Reciprocal: تَكَاتَبَ الصَّدِيقَانِ, "the two friends corresponded". تَشَاوَرَ, "to consult each other". The subject is plural or dual (or two subjects joined).

Pretending: تَمَارَضَ, tamāraḍa, "to feign illness"; تَجَاهَلَ, "to feign ignorance, ignore"; تَنَاوَمَ, "to pretend to sleep".

Gradual, step by step: تَزَايَدَ, "to increase gradually"; تَسَاقَطَ, "to fall one after another".

maṣdar تَفَاعُل: تَعَاوُن, تَفَاهُم.

## form VII: اِنْفَعَلَ

Passive or resultative of a transitive Form I. كَسَرَ, "to break (trans.)" → اِنْكَسَرَ, inkasara, "to break (intr.), get broken". فَتَحَ → اِنْفَتَحَ. صَرَفَ, "to turn away" → اِنْصَرَفَ, "to leave".

It is always intransitive, so it has no real passive and the passive participle is rare (only impersonal: مُنْصَرَف عَنْهُ). Not formed from roots whose first radical is ء و ي ل ن ر م — the n- would assimilate or clash. For those roots, Form VIII does the job: وَصَلَ → اِتَّصَلَ. [Haddad list; the book says "rarely", he says "never in practice"]

Form VII is not formed from roots whose first radical is {{c1::ء, و, ي, ل, ن, ر, م}}; Form VIII is used instead ^sf-vii-roots

## form VIII: اِفْتَعَلَ

Reflexive or middle of I, and the most common augmented form after II and IV. جَمَعَ, "to gather" → اِجْتَمَعَ, ijtamaʿa, "to meet, assemble". شَغَلَ, "to occupy" → اِشْتَغَلَ, "to work, be busy". نَظَرَ → اِنْتَظَرَ, "to wait". سَمِعَ → اِسْتَمَعَ, "to listen". Often lexicalized past any rule: اِحْتَرَمَ, "to respect".

The infixed tāʾ assimilates to the first radical. This is the single most tested thing in the course:

| first radical | what happens to the t | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ص ض ط ظ (emphatics) | t → ṭ | صَبَرَ → اِصْطَبَرَ; ضَرَبَ → اِضْطَرَبَ; طَلَعَ → اِطَّلَعَ; ظَلَمَ → اِظْطَلَمَ / اِظَّلَمَ |
| د ذ ز | t → d | دَعَا → اِدَّعَى; ذَكَرَ → اِذْدَكَرَ → اِدَّكَرَ (also اِذَّكَرَ); زَحَمَ → اِزْدَحَمَ |
| و ي | the weak letter becomes t, doubled | وَصَلَ → اِتَّصَلَ; وَفَقَ → اِتَّفَقَ; يَسَرَ → اِتَّسَرَ |
| ث | t → ṯ (or the ṯ → t) | ثَأَرَ → اِثَّأَرَ |
| ء (one verb) | irregular, like و | أَخَذَ → اِتَّخَذَ |

The Qurʾānic فَهَلْ مِنْ مُدَّكِرٍ (al-Qamar 54:17), "is there anyone who will take heed?": muddakir is the Form VIII active participle of ḏ-k-r with full assimilation ḏt → dd. Prof. Haddad's favorite example.

## form IX: اِفْعَلَّ

Colors and physical defects only, "to become X". اِحْمَرَّ, iḥmarra, "to turn red, blush"; اِصْفَرَّ, "to turn yellow, pale"; اِعْوَجَّ, "to become crooked"; اِعْوَرَّ, "to become one-eyed". The adjective is afʿal (أَحْمَر). Rare in MSA. The doubled final radical behaves like a doubled verb: اِحْمَرَّتْ, but اِحْمَرَرْتُ.

Form IX is used for {{c1::colors and physical defects}} (اِحْمَرَّ، اِعْوَجَّ) ^sf-ix

## form X: اِسْتَفْعَلَ

Seeking or asking for: غَفَرَ, "to forgive" → اِسْتَغْفَرَ, istaġfara, "to ask forgiveness". خَدَمَ, "to serve" → اِسْتَخْدَمَ, "to use (make serve)". فَهِمَ → اِسْتَفْهَمَ, "to inquire".

Considering something to be X: حَسُنَ, "to be good" → اِسْتَحْسَنَ, "to approve, find good". كَبُرَ → اِسْتَكْبَرَ, "to be haughty (consider oneself great)".

Becoming: حَجَرَ → اِسْتَحْجَرَ, "to petrify".

Hollow X: اِسْتَقَامَ يَسْتَقِيمُ اِسْتِقَامَة (same mechanics as IV iqāma). Defective X: اِسْتَدْعَى يَسْتَدْعِي اِسْتِدْعَاء.

Form X often means {{c1::seeking or asking for (اِسْتَغْفَرَ)}} or {{c2::considering something to be X (اِسْتَحْسَنَ)}} ^sf-x-meaning

## the passive

Internal vowel change, no auxiliary. The perfect passive is always u…i…a (u on every vowel before the last stem vowel, i on the last), the imperfect passive is yu-…a…u.

| form | active | passive perfect | passive imperfect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | كَتَبَ يَكْتُبُ | كُتِبَ | يُكْتَبُ |
| II | عَلَّمَ يُعَلِّمُ | عُلِّمَ | يُعَلَّمُ |
| III | قَاتَلَ يُقَاتِلُ | قُوتِلَ | يُقَاتَلُ |
| IV | أَخْرَجَ يُخْرِجُ | أُخْرِجَ | يُخْرَجُ |
| V | تَعَلَّمَ يَتَعَلَّمُ | تُعُلِّمَ | يُتَعَلَّمُ |
| VI | تَبَادَلَ يَتَبَادَلُ | تُبُودِلَ | يُتَبَادَلُ |
| VIII | اِسْتَمَعَ يَسْتَمِعُ | اُسْتُمِعَ | يُسْتَمَعُ |
| X | اِسْتَخْدَمَ يَسْتَخْدِمُ | اُسْتُخْدِمَ | يُسْتَخْدَمُ |

## identifying a form from a word (the exam skill)

Prof. Haddad's procedure, which got me through the final:

Strip known prefixes and suffixes (al-, -a, -ūna, -at, ta-, ya-, mu-…).

Look for the infixes: a t after the first radical (VIII), an n before it (VII), sta- (X), a long ā after the first radical (III, VI), a doubled middle radical (II, V).

Then the vowel of the imperfect prefix or the participle's mu- tells you whether the augment is from II/III/IV (yu-) or V+ (ya-). A word starting mut- is a V or VI participle; munf- is VII; must- is X.

Worked, from the final: مُتَعَاوِنُونَ → strip -ūna → mutaʿāwin → mu- + taʿāwin → ta- + ā after first radical → VI, root ʿ-w-n, active participle: "cooperating (people)". مُنْطَلَق → mu- + n before first radical → VII, ṭ-l-q, passive participle used as a noun: "starting point". اِسْتِقْبَال → sta- → X, q-b-l, maṣdar: "reception".

مُتَعَاوِنُونَ — root, form, and what kind of word?
?
root ع و ن; Form VI (ta- + long ā after the first radical); active participle, masc. plural: "cooperating people" ^sf-mutaawinun
