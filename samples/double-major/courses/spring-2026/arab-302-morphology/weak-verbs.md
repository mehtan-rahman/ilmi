## what "weak" means

A root is weak (مُعْتَلّ, muʿtall) when one of its radicals is و or ي, the حُرُوف العِلَّة (letters of weakness; alif is never a radical, it is always a w or y underneath, or a hamza). By position:

| name | Arabic | weak radical | example root | example verb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| assimilated | مِثَال (miṯāl) | first | و ص ل | وَصَلَ يَصِلُ |
| hollow | أَجْوَف (ajwaf) | second | ق و ل | قَالَ يَقُولُ |
| defective | نَاقِص (nāqiṣ) | third | ر م ي | رَمَى يَرْمِي |
| doubly weak, separated | لَفِيف مَفْرُوق | first and third | و ق ي | وَقَى يَقِي |
| doubly weak, joined | لَفِيف مَقْرُون | second and third | ط و ي | طَوَى يَطْوِي |

Not weak in this strict sense but taught in the same unit because they misbehave: doubled (مُضَعَّف, second and third the same) and hamzated (مَهْمُوز, a hamza anywhere). Prof. Haddad calls the whole set "the five families" (assimilated, hollow, defective, doubled, hamzated) and puts lafīf under defective.

[the term nāqiṣ for defective verbs and nāqiṣ for kāna and its sisters is the same word for two different things. Dr. Saeed made a point of it.]

a hollow verb (أَجْوَف) has a weak letter as its {{c1::second}} radical; a defective verb (نَاقِص) as its {{c2::third}} ^sf-ajwaf-naqis

## assimilated (مِثَال)

First radical wāw (rarely yāʾ). The rule that does all the work: in Form I, a wāw that would sit between the prefix vowel a and a kasra drops. So faʿala/yafʿilu verbs lose it in the imperfect and imperative:

وَصَلَ يَصِلُ, waṣala yaṣilu, "to arrive" (not *yawṣilu). Imperative صِلْ, ṣil.

وَعَدَ يَعِدُ, waʿada yaʿidu, "to promise". Imperative عِدْ.

وَقَفَ يَقِفُ, waqafa yaqifu, "to stop, stand". Imperative قِفْ.

وَجَدَ يَجِدُ, wajada yajidu, "to find".

وَضَعَ يَضَعُ, waḍaʿa yaḍaʿu, "to put". The imperfect has fatḥa because of the guttural ʿayn, but the original was yafʿilu, so the wāw still drops. Same for وَقَعَ يَقَعُ, وَهَبَ يَهَبُ.

faʿila/yafʿalu verbs keep the wāw: وَجِلَ يَوْجَلُ, wajila yawjalu, "to be afraid". And yāʾ-initial verbs keep the yāʾ: يَبِسَ يَيْبَسُ, yabisa yaybasu, "to dry up".

The maṣdar pattern فِعَة belongs to these verbs: صِلَة, عِدَة, جِهَة, صِفَة (from وَصَفَ), ثِقَة (from وَثِقَ). The wāw dropped and a tāʾ marbūṭa compensates. Also the regular ones: وُصُول, وَعْد.

Derived forms keep the wāw as a consonant or turn it into a long vowel: Form IV أَوْصَلَ يُوصِلُ إِيصَال (yu+w → ū; i+w → ī). Form X اِسْتَوْطَنَ يَسْتَوْطِنُ, "to settle". Form VIII assimilates it to t: اِتَّصَلَ (see verb forms).

in Form I, the wāw of an assimilated verb drops in the imperfect when the pattern is {{c1::faʿala / yafʿilu}} (وَصَلَ يَصِلُ), and stays in faʿila / yafʿalu (وَجِلَ يَوْجَلُ) ^sf-mithal-drop

## hollow (أَجْوَف)

Middle radical w or y, which in Form I appears as a long vowel (ā in the perfect, ū/ī/ā in the imperfect) and shortens whenever the syllable closes. That is the whole rule: long vowel in an open syllable, short in a closed one (CVVC is avoided).

Three types by imperfect vowel:

| type | root | perfect 3ms | perfect 1s | imperfect | jussive | imperative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| w, faʿala/yafʿulu | ق و ل | قَالَ qāla | قُلْتُ qultu | يَقُولُ yaqūlu | لَمْ يَقُلْ lam yaqul | قُلْ qul |
| y, faʿala/yafʿilu | ب ي ع | بَاعَ bāʿa | بِعْتُ biʿtu | يَبِيعُ yabīʿu | لَمْ يَبِعْ lam yabiʿ | بِعْ biʿ |
| w, faʿila/yafʿalu | خ و ف | خَافَ ḵāfa | خِفْتُ ḵiftu | يَخَافُ yaḵāfu | لَمْ يَخَفْ lam yaḵaf | خَفْ ḵaf |

The vowel in the 1s perfect tells you the type: u for wāw verbs of the qāla type, i for yāʾ verbs AND for faʿila verbs (ḵiftu, nimtu from نَامَ يَنَامُ). This is why kuntu (kāna) and ṣirtu (ṣāra, ṣ-y-r) differ.

Full perfect of قَالَ: قُلْتُ، قُلْتَ، قُلْتِ، قَالَ، قَالَتْ، قُلْنَا، قُلْتُمْ، قُلْتُنَّ، قَالُوا، قُلْنَ. The long ā shows up only where the ending begins with a vowel (or there is no ending): qāla, qālat (the -at's t is a closed syllable in pause but the stem syllable qā- is open), qālū. Everywhere else qul-.

Imperative plural and feminine: قُولِي qūlī, قُولُوا qūlū — the ending opens the syllable, the vowel is long again.

Participles: active فَاعِل with hamza in the middle: قَائِل, بَائِع, خَائِف. Passive: مَقُول, مَبِيع, مَخُوف (shortened from *maqwūl, *mabyūʿ).

Passive: قِيلَ qīla, بِيعَ bīʿa (perfect); يُقَالُ yuqālu, يُبَاعُ yubāʿu (imperfect).

Derived forms. II and III treat the w/y as a full consonant: قَوَّلَ, قَاوَمَ "to resist". IV, VII, VIII, X carry the long vowel:

Form IV أَقَامَ يُقِيمُ إِقَامَة, muqīm. Form VII اِنْقَادَ يَنْقَادُ اِنْقِيَاد, "to be led". Form VIII اِخْتَارَ يَخْتَارُ اِخْتِيَار, "to choose", muḵtār is both active and passive participle (muḵtayir and muḵtayar both collapse to muḵtār). Form X اِسْتَقَامَ يَسْتَقِيمُ اِسْتِقَامَة.

the jussive of خَافَ يَخَافُ is {{c1::لَمْ يَخَفْ}}: the long vowel shortens in the closed syllable ^sf-lam-yakhaf

## defective (نَاقِص)

Final radical w or y. Three types in Form I:

| type | root | perfect | imperfect | jussive | subjunctive | imperative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| w, faʿala/yafʿulu | د ع و | دَعَا daʿā | يَدْعُو yadʿū | لَمْ يَدْعُ lam yadʿu | لَنْ يَدْعُوَ lan yadʿuwa | اُدْعُ udʿu |
| y, faʿala/yafʿilu | ر م ي | رَمَى ramā | يَرْمِي yarmī | لَمْ يَرْمِ lam yarmi | لَنْ يَرْمِيَ lan yarmiya | اِرْمِ irmi |
| y, faʿila/yafʿalu | ل ق ي | لَقِيَ laqiya | يَلْقَى yalqā | لَمْ يَلْقَ lam yalqa | لَنْ يَلْقَى lan yalqā | اِلْقَ ilqa |

Spelling of the perfect 3ms: final alif (ا) for wāw roots, alif maqṣūra (ى) for yāʾ roots: دَعَا vs رَمَى. Same sound, different letter, and the 1st-person perfect gives the root away: دَعَوْتُ daʿawtu vs رَمَيْتُ ramaytu. faʿila verbs: لَقِيتُ laqītu.

Masculine plural perfect: دَعَوْا daʿaw, رَمَوْا ramaw, but لَقُوا laqū. The 3fs: دَعَتْ daʿat, رَمَتْ ramat (the final radical drops before -at), but لَقِيَتْ laqiyat.

Jussive: the final long vowel shortens (that is the "sukūn" of the jussive for these verbs): lam yadʿu, lam yarmi, lam yalqa. In iʿrāb: مَجْزُومٌ وَعَلَامَةُ جَزْمِهِ حَذْفُ حَرْفِ العِلَّةِ. Indicative yadʿū: marfūʿ bi-ḍamma muqaddara ʿalā l-wāw li-l-ṯiqal. [this is exactly the muqaddara business from 401 kāna unit, approached from the other side]

Subjunctive: fatḥa shows on w and y (lan yadʿuwa, lan yarmiya) but not on alif (lan yalqā, fatḥa muqaddara).

Participles: active فَاعٍ, a manqūṣ noun: دَاعٍ dāʿin, الدَّاعِي ad-dāʿī; رَامٍ rāmin. Passive: مَدْعُوّ madʿuww (w+w doubled), مَرْمِيّ marmiyy (w assimilates to y).

Maṣdar: دُعَاء, رَمْي, لِقَاء. Note the final hamza in duʿāʾ and liqāʾ: a weak letter after a long ā becomes hamza.

Derived forms all end in -ā in the perfect (spelled ى when the word has more than three letters, regardless of root) and in -ī (II, III, IV, VII, VIII, X) or -ā (V, VI) in the imperfect: سَمَّى يُسَمِّي, أَعْطَى يُعْطِي, اِشْتَرَى يَشْتَرِي, but تَمَنَّى يَتَمَنَّى, تَلَاقَى يَتَلَاقَى.

Maṣdars of derived defective verbs: II تَسْمِيَة; III مُلَاقَاة, لِقَاء; IV إِعْطَاء; V تَمَنٍّ; VIII اِشْتِرَاء; X اِسْتِدْعَاء.

## doubled (مُضَعَّف)

Second and third radicals the same. The rule: they merge (idġām) into one doubled consonant when the second of them would be followed by a vowel, and stay apart when it would carry sukūn.

مَدَّ يَمُدُّ, madda yamuddu, "to extend". مَدَّتْ maddat, مَدُّوا maddū — merged. مَدَدْتُ madadtu, مَدَدْنَا madadnā, مَدَدْنَ madadna — apart, since the d before -tu has sukūn.

Imperfect: يَمُدُّ, يَمُدُّونَ, but feminine plural يَمْدُدْنَ yamdudna.

Jussive and imperative: the final has sukūn, so they should separate, and they may: لَمْ يَمْدُدْ lam yamdud. Or they merge and add a helping vowel: لَمْ يَمُدَّ lam yamudda, لَمْ يَمُدِّ lam yamuddi (and with ḍamma-stem verbs also lam yamuddu). All correct. Imperative: اُمْدُدْ umdud or مُدَّ mudda / مُدِّ muddi. MSA prefers the merged forms.

Participles: مَادّ, مَمْدُود. maṣdar مَدّ.

Derived: Form IV أَحَبَّ يُحِبُّ, "to love" (and the maṣdar is usually حُبّ, the Form I noun, not إِحْبَاب). Form VIII اِمْتَدَّ يَمْتَدُّ اِمْتِدَاد. Form X اِسْتَعَدَّ يَسْتَعِدُّ اِسْتِعْدَاد, "to get ready". Form X perfect with consonant ending: اِسْتَعْدَدْتُ istaʿdadtu.

## hamzated (مَهْمُوز)

A hamza is a consonant like any other, so hamzated verbs are mostly regular in their sounds. What is irregular is (a) the spelling, the seat (kursī) the hamza sits on, and (b) a handful of very common verbs that lose the hamza.

The seat rules for a medial hamza: compare the hamza's own vowel with the vowel before it; the stronger wins, in the order kasra (seat ى) > ḍamma (seat و) > fatḥa (seat ا) > sukūn. سُئِلَ suʾila (kasra wins: ئ), يَسْأَلُ yasʾalu (fatḥa over sukūn: أ), رُؤُوس ruʾūs (ḍamma: ؤ; also written رُءُوس). Final hamza takes the seat of the vowel before it: قَرَأَ, يَقْرَأُ, بَطُؤَ, and sits on the line after a sukūn or long vowel: شَيْء, سَمَاء, ضَوْء.

The verbs that drop it:

أَكَلَ يَأْكُلُ, "to eat": imperative كُلْ kul.

أَخَذَ يَأْخُذُ, "to take": imperative خُذْ ḵuḏ.

أَمَرَ يَأْمُرُ, "to order": imperative مُرْ mur, but after wa- or fa- the full form returns: وَأْمُرْ wa-ʾmur.

سَأَلَ يَسْأَلُ, "to ask": imperative اِسْأَلْ isʾal, or سَلْ sal (Qurʾānic, سَلْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ).

رَأَى يَرَى, "to see": the hamza drops in the whole imperfect (yarā, not *yarʾā), jussive lam yara, imperative رَ ra (written رَهْ rah with hāʾ al-sakt when alone). raʾā is hamzated in the middle AND defective, the worst verb in the language. Form IV أَرَى يُرِي, "to show".

Initial hamza, Form IV and VIII: أَأْمَنَ → آمَنَ, "to believe" (two hamzas, the second becomes a long ā, written with madda). Form VIII of ʾ-m-n is اِئْتَمَنَ, "to entrust"; of ʾ-ḵ-ḏ the irregular اِتَّخَذَ.

## doubly weak (لَفِيف)

Each weak radical follows its own rule, and they stack.

لَفِيف مَفْرُوق (first and third): وَقَى يَقِي, waqā yaqī, "to protect". The wāw drops (assimilated rule), the final yāʾ behaves as defective. Jussive لَمْ يَقِ lam yaqi. Imperative: قِ qi, a one-letter word, written قِهْ qih in pause. Same: وَفَى يَفِي, "to fulfill" (imperative فِ), وَعَى يَعِي, "to be aware" (عِ).

لَفِيف مَقْرُون (second and third): طَوَى يَطْوِي, ṭawā yaṭwī, "to fold". The middle wāw is treated as a strong consonant; only the final one is weak. رَوَى يَرْوِي, "to narrate". نَوَى يَنْوِي, "to intend" (maṣdar نِيَّة).

the imperative of وَقَى يَقِي is {{c1::قِ (قِهْ in pause)}}, a single letter ^sf-qi

## the 401 connection (added Sept 2026)

kāna: hollow, wāw, qāla type: kuntu, yakūnu, lam yakun, kun.

ṣāra: hollow, yāʾ, bāʿa type: ṣirtu, yaṣīru, lam yaṣir.

bāta: hollow, yāʾ: bittu, yabītu.

dāma: hollow, wāw: dumtu, yadūmu.

zāla (nāqiṣ, "cease"): hollow, faʿila type: ziltu, yazālu. (zāla yazūlu "vanish" is the qāla type: zultu.)

aḍḥā, amsā: Form IV defective, aḍḥā yuḍḥī, amsā yumsī.

ẓalla: doubled, ẓalaltu (also ẓaltu, ẓiltu), yaẓallu.

fatiʾa: hamzated, faʿila, yaftaʾu; mā fatiʾa.

the imperfect of nāqiṣ zāla is {{c1::يَزَالُ (faʿila type, ziltu)}}, not يَزُولُ ^sf-yazalu
