Half-thought, probably wrong, walking back from Arabic: entropy and root-and-pattern morphology. A word like مَكْتُوب (maktūb, "written") is a root ك ت ب (k-t-b, the "writing" idea) poured into a pattern مَفْعُول (mafʿūl, passive participle). كَاتِب (kātib, "writer") is the same root in فَاعِل (fāʿil), مَكْتَب (maktab, "desk/office") in مَفْعَل (mafʿal). If roots and patterns combined freely, a word would carry $\log R + \log P$ bits. They don't: only some pairs are attested. So the attested lexicon is a subset of the root × pattern "phase space," and the gap between $\log(\text{attested pairs})$ and $\log R + \log P$ is a mutual information, the amount knowing the root tells you about which patterns are live. That part is just information theory and probably true. The part I want and can't have: the root as a macrostate and the patterns as its microstates, with "entropy of a root" = log of how many patterns it takes. Productive roots are high-entropy? Doesn't obviously mean anything. There's no dynamics, so no second law, so it's only a counting analogy. Stop here.
