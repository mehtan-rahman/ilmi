## idea for honors thesis

Something that is actually both majors instead of one of each.

Candidate: the concept of مَيْل (mayl, "inclination") in Arabic natural philosophy, read by someone who knows what momentum and impetus turned into. Ibn Sīnā (ابْن سِينَا) in the physics of *al-Šifāʾ* (الشِّفَاء): a thrown body moves because the thrower impresses a مَيْل قَسْرِيّ (mayl qasrī, "violent/forced inclination") on it, which is used up against the resistance of the medium; in a void it would not decay. That is uncomfortably close to a conserved quantity. Then أَبُو الْبَرَكَات الْبَغْدَادِيّ (Abū l-Barakāt al-Baghdādī), *Kitāb al-Muʿtabar*: a falling body's speed increases because the natural cause keeps producing new mayl, which reads like an argument for acceleration under a constant cause. [check all of this against secondary literature before saying it to anyone; I'm going from Saeed's seminar and one article.]

What I could add that a historian wouldn't: careful translation of the technical vocabulary (مَيْل, قُوَّة quwwa "power/force", حَرَكَة ḥaraka "motion"), plus a physicist's reading of what each claim would commit you to. The danger is the obvious one: reading Newton back into the text. A thesis that just says "Ibn Sīnā almost had momentum" is bad. A thesis that says exactly which modern statement each sentence is *compatible with* and which it rules out might be good.
