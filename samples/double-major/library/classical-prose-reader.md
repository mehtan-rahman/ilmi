The course reader for ARAB 301, *Readings in Classical Arabic Prose, with Commentary*, ed. Samīr Ḫalīl and Ruth Ashby (4th ed.). Forty short passages of adab prose in four graded parts, each passage printed fully vowelled, then a glossary, grammar notes keyed to the constructions in the passage, cultural notes, and a few exercises. The passages are composed or adapted by the editors "in the manner of" the anthologies (al-Tanūḫī, Ibn ʿAbd Rabbih, al-Jāḥiẓ's anecdotes, the *Faraj baʿd al-šidda* type) rather than quoted, so the vocalisation is clean and the grammar is graded. Dr. Saeed says this is a feature and a bug: good for learning the constructions, not a substitute for reading the real anthologies with their messy isnāds. Part IV drops the vowels on everything but the hard words.

How I'm using it: I import a passage the week we do it in class, read it incrementally (Arabic first, then the commentary, a few blocks a pass), carve the paragraphs I actually have to work on into extracts and do my translation and parsing there. Vocab goes into `arabic/vocabulary.md` under a heading per passage, not here, because I want one daily vocab queue. Root families go to `arabic/roots.md`.

The book is the editors' text. I don't edit the passages, only add cards under them and carve extracts.

Conventions in the commentary worth remembering: the editors write "ḥāl" for the circumstantial accusative and the circumstantial clause alike and then distinguish *ḥāl mufrad* (single word) from *jumlat ḥāl* (clause); they call كان and its sisters *al-nawāsiḫ* only once and then just "kāna-verbs"; transliteration is ALA-LC minus the case endings unless the note is about case.
