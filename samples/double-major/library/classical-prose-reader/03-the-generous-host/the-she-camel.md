فَقَالَتْ لَهُ امْرَأَتُهُ: «مَا تَصْنَعُ بِهِمْ وَلَيْسَ عِنْدَنَا إِلَّا هٰذِهِ النَّاقَةُ الَّتِي نَعِيشُ مِنْ لَبَنِهَا؟» فَقَالَ: «وَاللّٰهِ لَا يَبِيتُ ضَيْفِي جَائِعًا وَأَنَا حَيٌّ.» ثُمَّ قَامَ إِلَى النَّاقَةِ فَنَحَرَهَا، وَأَوْقَدَ النَّارَ، وَجَعَلَ يَشْوِي اللَّحْمَ وَيُقَدِّمُهُ إِلَيْهِمْ بِيَدِهِ حَتَّى شَبِعُوا.

## my translation (v1, 5 Mar, before class)

His wife said to him: "What are you doing with them, and there is nothing with us except this camel which we live from her milk?" He said: "By God my guest will not sleep hungry and I am alive." Then he stood to the camel and slaughtered her, and lit the fire, and made roasting the meat and presenting it to them with his hand until they were full.

## v2 (after class, 6 Mar)

His wife said to him, "What will you do for them, when all we have is this she-camel whose milk we live on?" "By God," he said, "no guest of mine will go to bed hungry while I live." Then he went to the camel and slaughtered her, lit a fire, and set about roasting the meat and serving it to them with his own hands until they had eaten their fill.

What changed: مَا تَصْنَعُ is future/modal not progressive ("what will you do"), and تَصْنَعُ بِـ is "do with/for" someone. قَامَ إِلَى is "got up and went to," not "stood to." جَعَلَ + imperfect is "began / set about" — I had "made," which is the full-verb meaning (جَعَلَهُ كَذَا, made it so). Prof. Lindqvist: "translate the *wāw al-ḥāl* with 'while' or 'when' and you will be right nine times out of ten, then learn the tenth."

## parsing

- امْرَأَتُهُ — fāʿil of قَالَتْ, nom.; the hamzat al-waṣl is not pronounced after لَهُ: *qālat lahu-mraʾatuhu*
- وَلَيْسَ عِنْدَنَا إِلَّا هٰذِهِ النَّاقَةُ — jumlat ḥāl; عِنْدَنَا khabar laysa muqaddam, النَّاقَةُ ism laysa muʾakhkhar, nom. (illā doesn't change the case when the negative sentence is "emptied," *istiṯnāʾ mufarraġ*)
- الَّتِي نَعِيشُ مِنْ لَبَنِهَا — relative clause on a definite noun so الَّتِي is needed; resumptive ـهَا on لَبَن
- وَاللّٰهِ — wāw al-qasam + gen.
- لَا يَبِيتُ — jawāb al-qasam, negative future
- جَائِعًا — ḥāl mufrad (or khabar of bāta — the book allows both)
- وَأَنَا حَيٌّ — jumlat ḥāl ismiyya: mubtadaʾ أَنَا + khabar حَيٌّ
- فَنَحَرَهَا — fa of sequence; ـهَا = the camel
- جَعَلَ يَشْوِي — fiʿl šurūʿ + imperfect = khabar; يَشْوِي defective, ending -ī in the indicative
- حَتَّى شَبِعُوا — perfect after ḥattā = end point reached

[asked in class: can it be وَأَنَا حَيًّا? — no, حَيٌّ is the khabar of a nominal sentence, it's only the whole clause that is "in the place of" an accusative ḥāl (*fī maḥall naṣb*)]

the case of حَيٌّ in وَأَنَا حَيٌّ is {{c1::nominative}}, because it is the {{c2::khabar of a nominal ḥāl clause}}; only the clause as a whole stands in the place of an accusative ^rd-ana-hayy
