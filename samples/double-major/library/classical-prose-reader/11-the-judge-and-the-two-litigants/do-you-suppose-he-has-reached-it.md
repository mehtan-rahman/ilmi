فَمَضَى الرَّجُلُ، وَجَلَسَ الآخَرُ بَيْنَ يَدَيِ القَاضِي سَاكِتًا. فَأَقْبَلَ القَاضِي عَلَى غَيْرِهِ مِنَ الخُصُومِ يَنْظُرُ فِي أُمُورِهِمْ، حَتَّى إِذَا مَضَتْ سَاعَةٌ الْتَفَتَ إِلَيْهِ فَقَالَ لَهُ، كَأَنَّهُ يَسْأَلُ عَنْ أَمْرٍ هَيِّنٍ: «أَتُرَاهُ بَلَغَ الشَّجَرَةَ بَعْدُ؟» قَالَ: «لَا، إِنَّهَا بَعِيدَةٌ مِنْ هُنَا.»

This is the paragraph the whole story turns on, and the one Dr. Saeed made us read aloud three times "until the trap is audible."

## translation, v1 (17 Sept)

So the man went off, and the other sat silent before the judge. The judge turned to the other litigants, looking into their cases, until, when an hour had passed, he turned to him and said to him, as if asking about a small matter: "Do you see him he reached the tree yet?" He said, "No, it is far from here."

## v2 (after OH, 22 Sept)

The man went off, and the other sat in silence before the judge. The judge turned his attention to other litigants and looked into their affairs, until, after a while, he turned to him and asked him casually, as though it were a matter of no importance, "Do you suppose he has reached the tree yet?" "No," said the man, "it's a long way from here."

Things I got wrong in v1:

- أَتُرَاهُ is not "do you see him" — passive *turā*, "are you made to think" = do you suppose. If it were active it would be أَتَرَاهُ (*a-tarāhu*), which would be "do you see him," and then the question would be absurd (the man is gone). The vowelling is the whole difference: tu- vs ta-. [this is why the reader vowels everything]
- سَاعَة is "a while" not "an hour" in this register.
- حَتَّى إِذَا … الْتَفَتَ: main clause has no fa. I'd mentally inserted one.
- "casually" for كَأَنَّهُ يَسْأَلُ عَنْ أَمْرٍ هَيِّنٍ — Dr. Saeed: fine as a translation, but in a parsing exam give the literal version.

## parsing

- فَمَضَى — māḍī of مَضَى (defective, alif maqṣūra)
- بَيْنَ يَدَيِ القَاضِي — dual يَدَانِ in iḍāfa → يَدَيْ, kasra helping vowel before al-
- سَاكِتًا — ḥāl mufrad on الآخَرُ
- يَنْظُرُ فِي أُمُورِهِمْ — jumlat ḥāl fiʿliyya, no wāw (imperfect ḥāl takes no wāw when affirmative)
- حَتَّى إِذَا مَضَتْ سَاعَةٌ — ḥattā idhā + perf; مَضَتْ fem. for سَاعَة
- كَأَنَّهُ يَسْأَلُ — kaʾanna + ism ـهُ + khabar (verbal sentence)
- أَتُرَاهُ — hamza istifhām + تُرَى (passive impf 2ms of رَأَى) + ـهُ first object
- بَلَغَ الشَّجَرَةَ — in place of second object
- بَعْدُ — mabnī ʿalā al-ḍamm, complement cut off
- إِنَّهَا بَعِيدَةٌ — inna + ism ـهَا + khabar; the slip: he knows the distance

[ask in OH: is the imperfect ḥāl ever with wāw? — answered 22 Sept: with a negated imperfect (وَلَا يَنْظُرُ) it can be; affirmative imperfect ḥāl normally without. Exceptions exist with a pronoun subject: وَهُوَ يَنْظُرُ]
