The imperative of أَتَى "to come" is ائْتِ, with a *hamzat al-waṣl* and the radical *hamza* resting on a *yāʾ* seat. When it is preceded by وَ or فَ the connective *hamza* falls away in pronunciation and in spelling, and the radical *hamza* moves to an *alif* seat: وَأْتِ, *waʾti*. With the suffix of the first person this gives وَأْتِنِي, *waʾtinī*, "and bring me."

My own working through it, because I kept writing وَائْتِنِي on the quiz:

The underlying imperative is *iʾti* (اِئْتِ): the prosthetic *i-* of the hamzat al-waṣl, then the radical hamza with sukūn. Hamza with sukūn after kasra sits on a yāʾ: ئْ. After wa- the prosthetic syllable is not pronounced at all (the wa supplies the vowel), so the word is *wa-ʾti*: hamza with sukūn after fatḥa sits on alif: أْ. Spelling follows pronunciation here, and the alif of waṣl is dropped in writing too, which is unusual (normally the waṣl alif stays written, e.g. وَاذْهَبْ). The reason it goes: two alifs in a row (ا + أ) are never written.

Paradigm, so I have it in one place:

- ائْتِ / فَأْتِ / وَأْتِ — 2ms
- ائْتِي / فَأْتِي — 2fs
- ائْتُوا / فَأْتُوا — 2mp, as in وَأْتُوا البُيُوتَ مِنْ أَبْوَابِهَا
- ائْتِنِي بِـ — bring me …
- the same with أَمَرَ in some texts: وَأْمُرْ (Q 20:132 has وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِالصَّلَاةِ)

Compare the verbs that drop the hamza entirely: خُذْ، كُلْ، مُرْ (from أَخَذَ، أَكَلَ، أَمَرَ). أَمَرَ does both: مُرْ at the start of speech, وَأْمُرْ after wa. [check: is وَمُرْ also attested? Dr. Saeed said yes, both]
