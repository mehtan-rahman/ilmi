قَالَ: «يَا ابْنَ أَخِي، إِنِّي خَرَجْتُ مِنْ بَيْتِي وَمَا أَمْلِكُ شَيْئًا، فَكَسَبْتُ هٰذَا المَالَ بِالأَسْفَارِ، ثُمَّ ذَهَبَ كَمَا جَاءَ. وَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ المَالَ عَارِيَّةٌ، وَأَنَّ العَارِيَّةَ مَرْدُودَةٌ. وَبَقِيَ لِي مَا لَا يَأْخُذُهُ لِصٌّ.» قُلْتُ: «وَمَا هُوَ؟» قَالَ: «عَقْلِي الَّذِي كَسَبْتُ بِهِ المَالَ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ، وَيَدَايَ اللَّتَانِ عَمِلْتُ بِهِمَا.»

## translation attempt (26 Oct, not yet checked in class)

He said, "Nephew, I left my house owning nothing, and I earned this money by travelling, and then it went the way it came. I have learnt that wealth is a loan, and a loan is given back. And there remains to me what no thief can take." "And what is that?" I said. He said, "My mind, with which I earned the money the first time, and my two hands that I worked with."

Unsure:

- يَا ابْنَ أَخِي — "nephew" or "my boy"? It's not literal, he's not his uncle. "My son" is too much (that'd be يَا بُنَيَّ). Going with "my boy" maybe. [ask Dr. Saeed Tue]
- ذَهَبَ كَمَا جَاءَ — "easy come easy go" is too slangy. "went as it came."
- مَرْدُودَةٌ — passive participle, "returned" / "must be returned." The participle can have the modal sense? #TODO: check — the commentary says "to be returned" so yes.
- أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ — adverbial acc., "the first time" ✓

## parsing

- إِنِّي — inna + ـي (ism); khabar the verbal sentence خَرَجْتُ…
- وَمَا أَمْلِكُ شَيْئًا — jumlat ḥāl, negated impf, with wāw
- بِالأَسْفَارِ — بِـ of means; أَسْفَار pl. of سَفَر
- وَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ — qad + perf. = "I have (come to) know" (perfect of knowing ≈ present state)
- أَنَّ المَالَ عَارِيَّةٌ — ism anna acc., khabar nom.
- مَا لَا يَأْخُذُهُ لِصٌّ — mā relative = fāʿil of بَقِيَ; لِصٌّ fāʿil of يَأْخُذُ; ـهُ resumptive
- وَمَا هُوَ — mā interrogative mubtadaʾ, هُوَ khabar (or the reverse — book doesn't say)
- عَقْلِي — khabar of understood هُوَ
- الَّذِي كَسَبْتُ بِهِ — بِهِ resumptive with preposition
- يَدَايَ — nom. dual + yāʾ; nūn drops
- اللَّتَانِ — fem. dual relative nom.
